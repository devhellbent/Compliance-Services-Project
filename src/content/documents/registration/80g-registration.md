80G Registration

Overview

80G registration, governed by Section 80G of the Income Tax Act, 1961, is a certification obtained by charitable organizations in India, such as trusts, societies, and Section 8 companies. Its primary function is to provide a crucial incentive for donors: the ability to claim a deduction from their taxable income on the donations they make to the registered entity. This effectively lowers the donor's tax liability and encourages philanthropic contributions.

The registration is beneficial for both the donor and the organization. For the donor, the deduction can range from 50% to 100% of the donated amount, depending on the recipient fund or institution, subject to certain limits. For the non-profit organization, holding an 80G certificate significantly enhances its credibility and transparency, making it far more appealing to potential individual, corporate, and foreign donors, thereby boosting fundraising potential.

To be eligible for 80G certification, an organization must fulfill several conditions, including possessing a valid Section 12A registration (which grants tax exemption on the organization's income), maintaining transparent accounts, and ensuring its income is used solely for charitable purposes. The application is made to the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner of Income Tax, and the registration must be periodically renewed to remain valid.

What is Section 80G

Section 80G of the Indian Income Tax Act offers tax deductions to taxpayers (individuals, companies, etc.) who make donations to specified charitable institutions or relief funds. This provision encourages philanthropy by allowing donors to deduct a percentage (usually 50% or 100%) of the donated amount from their taxable income, thereby reducing their tax liability.

Features

Features of 80G Registration

The main features of Section 80G Registration (for charitable organizations in India) revolve around providing tax incentives to donors and establishing the organization's credibility.

Here are the key features:

Tax Deduction for Donors: This is the core feature. Organizations with 80G registration can offer their donors a deduction of 50% or 100% of the donated amount from their taxable income, depending on the nature of the fund or institution. This acts as a major incentive for donations.

Enhanced Credibility: The registration is an official recognition by the Income Tax Department, signifying that the organization adheres to legal compliance. This significantly increases trust among individual and corporate donors.

Eligibility for Corporate Donations (CSR): Having 80G and the related 12A registration is often a mandatory requirement for NGOs to receive funding under Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) projects.

Charitable Purpose Mandate: The organization's assets and income must be used solely for charitable purposes, not for the private benefit of its founders or members.

No Religious Discrimination (General Rule): The organization should generally not be working for the benefit of a particular religious community or caste to qualify for the full benefits.

Mandatory Documentation and Compliance: The NGO must maintain regular and transparent accounts of its receipts and expenditures, and issue proper receipts to donors for them to claim the tax deduction.

Provisional and Final Validity: New registrations are typically granted a provisional validity of three years, which must then be converted into a final approval, generally valid for five years before renewal.

Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages of 80G Registration (for the NGO)

The primary benefit is attracting more funds by offering tax incentives to donors.

Attracts More Donors: This is the biggest advantage. Donors (individuals and corporations) prefer contributing to an 80G-registered organization because it allows them to claim a tax deduction (usually 50% of the donation amount, sometimes 100%) from their taxable income.

Enhances Credibility and Goodwill: Official recognition by the Income Tax Department signals transparency and compliance, significantly boosting the organization's reputation and trust among the general public and stakeholders.

Eligibility for Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) Funds: Many corporate donors, especially those providing CSR funds, mandate 80G registration as a prerequisite for funding.

Access to Government/Foreign Grants: While 12A registration is generally mandatory for tax exemption on income, 80G status often makes an organization more competitive and eligible for various government schemes and potentially foreign contributions.

Boosts Fundraising: By providing a dual benefit (supporting a cause and tax savings), the organization can run more effective fundraising campaigns and receive larger donations.

Disadvantages and Compliance Challenges of 80G Registration

The drawbacks mainly relate to the stringent compliance requirements and restrictions placed on the organization's operations.

Strict Compliance Burden: The organization must adhere to strict rules set by the Income Tax Department, including:

Maintaining separate and accurate books of accounts.

Mandatory annual reporting of donations received (Form 10BD) and issuing certificates to donors (Form 10BE).

Restriction on Use of Funds: The organization's income and assets must be used only for charitable purposes. Any diversion for non-charitable use or personal benefit of trustees/members can lead to cancellation of the certificate.

Restrictions on Activities: The organization must not be working for the benefit of any specific religious community or caste.

Mandatory Re-Registration and Validity Period:

The registration is not permanent anymore. After an initial provisional period, the final approval is typically valid for 5 years and must be renewed by applying at least six months prior to expiry.

Failure to renew the certificate on time leads to the loss of 80G benefits, which instantly deters donors.

Rejection Risk in New Regime: Organizations that receive provisional registration and later fail to secure final registration may face uncertainty, potentially impacting the tax-exempt status of past donations, which can cause donor backlash.

Eligibility

Eligibility Criteria for 80G Registration

The eligibility criteria for 80G registration are primarily focused on the donee organization (the one receiving the donation) to ensure it is genuinely charitable, compliant with income tax laws, and serves the public good.

An organization must meet several conditions under the Income Tax Act, 1961 (India), to get and retain its 80G approval.

To be eligible for 80G registration, an institution or fund must satisfy the following key conditions:

Legal and Operational Status

Legal Structure: The organization must be legally registered as a Charitable Trust, Society, or a Section 8 Company (Non-profit company) under the relevant Indian laws.

Mandatory Registration: It must have a valid registration under Section 12A/12AB of the Income Tax Act, which grants the organization tax-exempt status on its income. 12A/12AB registration is a prerequisite for 80G.

Charitable Objective: The primary objective and activities of the institution must be charitable as defined under the Income Tax Act, not profit-oriented.

Financial and Accounting Compliance

Proper Accounts: The organization must maintain regular books of accounts for its receipts and expenditures.

Audited Accounts: It must file audited financial statements (Balance Sheet, Income & Expenditure Account) annually.

Use of Income: The income and assets of the organization must be applied solely for charitable or religious purposes within India.

Restrictions on Activities

No Business Income (Generally): The organization should generally not have any business income. If it does have business income, it must maintain separate books of accounts, and the donations received must not be diverted to that business.

No Private Benefit: No part of the income or assets should be used for the benefit of the founder, governing body members, or their relatives.

No Religious/Caste Specificity: The institution must not be established for the exclusive benefit of any particular religious community or caste.

No Political Activities: The organization must not engage in any political activities.

Documents

Documents required for 80G Registration

Certainly! The application for 80G registration requires several key documents, most of which must be self-certified copies.

Here is a comprehensive list of the typical documents required, categorized for clarity:

Statutory and Foundation Documents

These documents establish the legal identity and structure of the organization:

Incorporation/Registration Proof: A self-certified copy of the Registration Certificate issued by the relevant authority (e.g., Registrar of Societies, Registrar of Companies, or Charity Commissioner).

Constitutional Document:

For a Trust: Self-certified copy of the Trust Deed.

For a Society/Section 8 Company: Self-certified copy of the Memorandum of Association (MOA) and Articles of Association (AOA)/Bylaws.

PAN Card: A self-certified copy of the organization's PAN Card.

12AB Certificate: A self-certified copy of the existing order granting registration under Section 12AB (or the older 12A/12AA). This registration is mandatory.

Financial and Activity Documents

These documents demonstrate the organization's compliance, financial status, and charitable work:

Financial Statements: Self-certified copies of the Annual Accounts (Balance Sheet, Income & Expenditure Account, and Audit Report) for the period not exceeding 3 financial years immediately preceding the year of application (if the organization has been in existence).

Bank Statements: Copies of the organization's Bank Statements for the preceding years (usually 3 years).

Activity Report: A detailed note on the activities carried out since the organization's inception or during the last three years, whichever is shorter.

Business Income Details (if applicable): If the NGO has business income (which must be incidental to its main objective), separate books of accounts and the audit report under Section 44AB are required.

List of Donors: Details of the major donors who contributed during the last three years, including their name, address, and PAN (often requested during the scrutiny process).

Administrative Documents

These pertain to the physical location and governance of the organization:

Address Proof: A copy of the utility bill (electricity, water, property tax receipt, etc.) for the registered office address.

NOC (No-Objection Certificate): Required from the landlord if the registered office space is rented.

Governing Body Details: A list of members of the Governing Body/Trustees/Directors with their full names, addresses, and PAN numbers.

DARPAN ID: Registration details with the NITI Aayog DARPAN Portal are often required, especially for organizations seeking government grants.

Fees

Fees of 80G Registration in India

The fee structure for 80G Registration in India can be divided into two components: the official government fee and the professional consultation fee.

Official Government Fee

There is no official government fee or statutory fee charged by the Income Tax Department for filing the application for 80G registration.

The application for both provisional and regular 80G (and the prerequisite 12AB) registration is filed electronically on the Income Tax e-filing portal using Form 10A or Form 10AB, and the department does not charge a processing fee for this.

Professional/Consultation Fees

Since the registration process involves detailed documentation, compliance checks, and online filing on the Income Tax portal, most NGOs hire professionals (like Chartered Accountants or consultants) to handle the process.

Fee Range: The professional fee charged by consultants, CAs, or legal firms for preparing the documents and filing the 12AB and 80G applications typically ranges from ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 or more, depending on:

The complexity and age of the organization.

Whether the NGO is applying for both 12AB and 80G together (which is common).

The professional's experience and location.

Whether the fee includes the preparation of the requisite documents like the Trust Deed/MOA or past financial statements (if needed).
