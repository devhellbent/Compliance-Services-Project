Secretarial Audit

OVERVIEW

A Secretarial Audit is an independent compliance audit conducted by a Practicing Company Secretary (PCS) to verify a company's adherence to corporate laws, SEBI regulations, FEMA, and other statutory requirements. Acting as a "legal health check," it ensures that the company’s non-financial operations—such as board meetings, share transfers, and regulatory filings—are legally sound and transparent.

Features of Secretarial Audit

*   **Independent Verification:** Conducted by an external Practicing Company Secretary (PCS) to ensure objectivity.
*   **Comprehensive Legal Scope:** Covers the Companies Act, SEBI, FEMA, and industry-specific regulations.
*   **Report in Form MR-3:** The auditor issues a formal report (Form MR-3) which is annexed to the Board’s Report.
*   **Systemic Review:** Focuses on procedural compliance, minute-keeping, and the effectiveness of internal governance.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Identifies procedural gaps and potential legal liabilities before they escalate into penalties.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Secretarial Audit

### Advantages
*   **Liability Protection:** Protects Directors and KMPs by providing evidence of due diligence and legal compliance.
*   **Investor Confidence:** Enhances credibility with investors, PE firms, and banks by demonstrating high governance standards.
*   **Early Warning System:** Identifies "Officers in Default" and procedural lapses early, preventing heavy compounding fees.
*   **Streamlined M&A:** Simplifies legal due diligence during mergers, acquisitions, or IPOs.
*   **ESG Compliance:** Supports Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) scores by proving the company is a "Good Corporate Citizen."

### Disadvantages
*   **Professional Cost:** Requires payment of professional fees to a Practicing Company Secretary (PCS).
*   **Administrative Effort:** Demands significant time and effort from the secretarial team to prepare and present records for audit.
*   **Detailed Scrutiny:** May uncover historical lapses that require expensive "compounding" or "rectification" to resolve.

Eligible Criteria for Secretarial Audit

Under Section 204 of the Companies Act, 2013, Secretarial Audit is mandatory for the following entities:

*   **Listed Companies:** Every company whose securities are listed on a recognized stock exchange.
*   **Public Companies (Unlisted):**
    *   Paid-up Share Capital: ₹50 Crore or more.
    *   Turnover: ₹250 Crore or more.
*   **High Borrowing Companies:** Any company (including private firms) with outstanding loans or borrowings from banks/PFIs of ₹100 Crore or more.
*   **Material Subsidiaries:** Any unlisted material Indian subsidiary of a listed company.

Documents Required for Secretarial Audit

*   **Charter Documents:** Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Association (MoA), and Articles of Association (AoA).
*   **Statutory Registers:** Register of Members (MGT-1), Directors & KMPs (MGT-10), Charges (CHG-7), and Loans/Investments (MBP-2).
*   **Meeting Records:** Notices, Agendas, and signed Minute Books of Board, Committee, and General Meetings.
*   **Filings:** Annual Returns (MGT-7), Financial Statements (AOC-4), and event-based forms (PAS-3, DIR-12, MGT-14).
*   **Director Disclosures:** Form MBP-1 (Disclosure of Interest) and Form DIR-8 (Non-disqualification).
*   **Compliance Certificates:** Proof of PF/ESI payments, POSH reports, and industry-specific licenses.

Legal Due Diligence

OVERVIEW

Legal Due Diligence (LDD) is a rigorous investigation into a company’s legal health, typically conducted before a merger, acquisition, or major investment. It involves a systematic review of corporate records, material contracts, intellectual property, and pending litigation to uncover hidden liabilities. By verifying the truth of a seller’s claims, LDD ensures that investors or acquirers are fully aware of any legal risks that could impact the deal's valuation or future operations.

Features of Legal Due Diligence

*   **Risk Categorization:** Uses a color-coded "Risk Matrix" (Red, Amber, Green) to prioritize issues by severity and impact.
*   **Module-Based Review:** Systematic audit across Corporate Governance, IP, Material Contracts, Litigation, and HR.
*   **Executive Summary:** Provides a "Bottom Line" assessment of deal viability for quick decision-making.
*   **Virtual Data Room (VDR):** Leverages secure digital repositories for organized document inspection and audit trails.
*   **Forward-Looking Analysis:** Identifies "Change of Control" triggers and integration risks that could affect the post-deal phase.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Legal Due Diligence

### Advantages
*   **Pricing Power:** Provides the buyer with "hard facts" to negotiate price reductions or create escrow accounts for potential liabilities.
*   **Indemnification Protection:** Helps draft precise "Warranties and Indemnities" (W&Is) in the share purchase agreement.
*   **Verification of IP Assets:** Confirms that critical patents, trademarks, and software code are legally owned by the company, not individuals.
*   **Clean Title Assurance:** Verifies that property and assets are free from undisclosed encumbrances or mortgages.
*   **Regulatory Shield:** Ensures compliance with complex laws like the DPDPA (Data Privacy) and FEMA (Foreign Investment).

### Disadvantages
*   **High Professional Costs:** Requires specialized legal firms, leading to significant transactional expenses.
*   **Time-Intensive:** Can take several weeks to months, potentially slowing down the deal momentum.
*   **Disclosure Risk:** For the seller, it exposes internal weaknesses and sensitive commercial information to external parties.

Eligible Criteria for Legal Due Diligence

Legal Due Diligence is typically triggered by major corporate milestones rather than mandatory annual cycles. Key scenarios include:

*   **Fundraising:** Required by VCs or PE firms before releasing Series A, B, or C funding.
*   **Mergers & Acquisitions:** Essential for both the buyer (to assess risk) and the seller (to prepare for audit).
*   **IPO Readiness:** Mandatory for companies planning to list on stock exchanges (NSE/BSE) to prepare the DRHP.
*   **Joint Ventures:** Required to ensure both partners have valid titles to their respective assets.
*   **Cross-Border Deals:** Any transaction involving Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) must undergo LDD for FEMA compliance.

Documents Required for Legal Due Diligence

*   **Corporate Records:** Certificate of Incorporation, MoA, AoA, and signed Minutes of Board and Shareholder meetings.
*   **Capital Structure:** Updated Cap Table, ESOP plans, and details of any convertible notes or warrants.
*   **Material Contracts:** Top 10 customer/vendor agreements, lease deeds, and joint venture contracts.
*   **Intellectual Property:** Registration certificates for Trademarks, Patents, and Copyrights; IP assignment agreements.
*   **Financing Documents:** Loan agreements, sanction letters, and records of charges created on assets.
*   **Litigation & Compliance:** List of pending civil/criminal/tax cases, GST/Income Tax filings, and DPDPA privacy policies.
*   **HR & Labor:** Employment contract templates, POSH policy, and proof of PF/ESI/Gratuity payments.

Corporate Governance

OVERVIEW

Corporate Governance is the framework of rules, relationships, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled. It ensures ethical leadership and balances the interests of a company's many stakeholders, including shareholders, management, customers, suppliers, and the community. By establishing a "system of checks and balances," it fosters long-term sustainability and builds trust in the capital markets.

Features of Corporate Governance

*   **Ethical Leadership:** Establishes a "Tone at the Top" based on integrity, transparency, and accountability.
*   **Board Oversight:** Defines the roles of Independent Directors and Committees (Audit, NRC, SRC) in monitoring management.
*   **Stakeholder Balance:** Protects the rights of minority shareholders and ensures equitable treatment for all investors.
*   **Risk Management:** Integrates internal controls and predictive oversight to prevent fraud and corporate scandals.
*   **Transparent Disclosure:** Mandates timely and accurate reporting of financial results and material information.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Corporate Governance

### Advantages
*   **Lower Cost of Capital:** Banks and lenders offer better rates to well-governed firms due to lower default risks.
*   **Higher Valuation:** Companies with robust governance frameworks often command significant premiums in the stock market.
*   **Talent Retention:** Ethical leadership and a "Speak-Up" culture attract and retain top-tier professionals.
*   **Regulatory Compliance:** Avoids massive penalties under the Companies Act, SEBI LODR, and data privacy laws.
*   **Long-term Sustainability:** Ensures the company survives market shocks by prioritizing purpose over short-term profits.

### Disadvantages
*   **Administrative Complexity:** Requires setting up multiple committees and maintaining rigorous documentation.
*   **Increased Costs:** Involves professional fees for board evaluations, independent directors, and governance audits.
*   **Slower Decision-Making:** The process of committee reviews and board approvals can sometimes slow down executive actions.

Eligible Criteria for Corporate Governance

While every company should practice good governance, specific statutory requirements apply to:

*   **Listed Companies:** Mandatory compliance with SEBI (LODR) Regulations, including board composition and committee requirements.
*   **Large Public Companies:** Mandatory appointment of Independent Directors and formation of Audit and NRC committees (based on capital/turnover thresholds).
*   **Regulated Entities:** Banks, NBFCs, and Insurance companies must follow specific RBI/IRDAI governance guidelines.
*   **Foreign Subsidiaries:** Multi-national companies often mandate "Global Governance Standards" for their Indian entities.

Documents Required for Corporate Governance

*   **Corporate Policies:** Code of Conduct, Whistleblower Policy, CSR Policy, and Insider Trading Code.
*   **Board Documentation:** Letter of Appointment for Independent Directors, Board Evaluation Reports, and Committee Charters.
*   **Meeting Records:** Notices, Agendas, and signed Minutes of Board and Committee meetings.
*   **Compliance Reports:** Annual Corporate Governance Report (for listed firms) and Secretarial Audit Reports (Form MR-3).
*   **Disclosures:** MBP-1 (Interest of Directors) and DIR-8 (Non-disqualification) certificates.

Internal Audit & Process Review

OVERVIEW

Internal Audit is an independent, objective assurance and consulting activity designed to add value and improve an organization's operations. It helps a company accomplish its objectives by bringing a systematic, disciplined approach to evaluate and improve the effectiveness of risk management, control, and governance processes. While Internal Audit focuses on compliance and risk, a Process Review acts as an efficiency engine, scrutinizing workflows to eliminate bottlenecks and wastage.

Features of Internal Audit & Process Review

*   **Risk-Based Approach:** Prioritizes high-risk areas like financial leakage, data security, and regulatory non-compliance.
*   **Operational Scrutiny:** Conducts "end-to-end" reviews of core cycles (Procure-to-Pay, Order-to-Cash, Payroll).
*   **Independent Assurance:** Provides the Audit Committee and Board with an objective view of the company’s internal control environment.
*   **Efficiency Mapping:** Identifies redundant steps and "bottlenecks" in business processes to improve productivity.
*   **Statutory Compliance:** Verifies adherence to the Companies Act (Section 138), FEMA, GST, and the DPDP Act.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Internal Audit & Process Review

### Advantages
*   **Fraud Prevention:** Detects financial irregularities and "ghost transactions" through regular transaction testing.
*   **Cost Savings:** Identifies operational wastage and suggests "leaner" workflows, directly impacting the bottom line.
*   **Investor Confidence:** A strong internal audit function signals professional management to PE firms and lenders.
*   **Early Warning System:** Proactively identifies emerging risks, such as cybersecurity gaps or supply chain disruptions.
*   **Data Integrity:** Ensures that management reports (MIS) are accurate, enabling better strategic decision-making.

### Disadvantages
*   **Professional Fees:** Involves recurring costs for internal audit teams or external consulting firms.
*   **Operational Disruption:** The audit process requires significant time from department heads and staff for data collection and interviews.
*   **Implementation Resistance:** Employees may resist changes suggested during process reviews, leading to cultural friction.

Eligible Criteria for Internal Audit

Under Section 138 of the Companies Act, 2013, Internal Audit is mandatory for the following classes of companies:

*   **Listed Companies:** Every listed company.
*   **Unlisted Public Companies:**
    *   Paid-up Share Capital: ₹50 Crore or more.
    *   Turnover: ₹200 Crore or more.
    *   Outstanding Loans/Borrowings: ₹100 Crore or more.
    *   Outstanding Deposits: ₹25 Crore or more.
*   **Private Companies:**
    *   Turnover: ₹200 Crore or more.
    *   Outstanding Loans/Borrowings: ₹100 Crore or more.

Documents Required for Internal Audit & Process Review

*   **Governance Folder:** Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), Organization Charts, and IT Security Policies.
*   **Financial Data:** General Ledger extracts, Accounts Payable (AP) invoices, and Purchase Orders (POs).
*   **HR & Payroll:** Employee Master files, Payroll registers, and proof of statutory filings (PF/ESI/PT).
*   **Operational Logs:** Inventory movement records, Material Contracts with top vendors, and Logistics reports.
*   **IT & Security Artifacts:** User access logs, backup records, and data processing consent forms (DPDPA).
*   **Previous Reports:** Past internal audit findings and management response tracking sheets.
