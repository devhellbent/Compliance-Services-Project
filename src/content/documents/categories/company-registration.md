### 1. Overview

Proprietorship is the simplest and most common form of business in India, owned and managed by a single individual. It has no separate legal entity, meaning the owner and the business are considered the same. This structure is ideal for solo entrepreneurs, freelancers, and small business owners who want complete control with minimal regulatory formalities and low setup costs.

### 2. Features

*   **Single Ownership:** Owned and controlled entirely by one individual.
*   **No Separate Entity:** The business and the proprietor are legally one and the same.
*   **Complete Control:** The owner makes all decisions without requiring consensus or board approval.
*   **Direct Profits:** All profits generated by the business belong solely to the proprietor.
*   **Minimal Compliance:** Fewer legal and regulatory obligations compared to corporate structures.
*   **Unlimited Liability:** The proprietor is personally responsible for all business debts and losses.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Ease of Setup:** Involves minimal documentation and can be started quickly.
*   **Cost-Effective:** Low formation and maintenance costs due to fewer filings.
*   **Operational Flexibility:** Quick decision-making and easy adaptation to market changes.
*   **Confidentiality:** Financial records are not required to be made public.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Personal Risk:** Personal assets can be seized to pay off business liabilities.
*   **Limited Capital:** Difficult to raise funds as it cannot issue shares or attract investors.
*   **Limited Lifespan:** The business may cease to exist if the owner dies or becomes incapacitated.
*   **Growth Constraints:** Dependent solely on the skills and resources of one individual.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Age:** The proprietor must be at least 18 years old.
*   **Citizenship:** Must be a citizen and resident of India.
*   **Business Name:** Must be unique and not infringe on existing trademarks.
*   **Lawful Activity:** The business must be legal and not prohibited by any law.
*   **Address:** Must have a valid address proof for the business operations.

### 5. Documents

*   **Proprietor KYC:** PAN Card, Aadhaar Card, and recent passport-sized photographs.
*   **Business Address Proof:** Electricity bill, property tax receipt, or rent agreement with an NOC from the landlord.
*   **Bank Account Proof:** Cancelled cheque or bank statement (post-registration).
*   **Optional Registrations:** MSME/Udyam Registration certificate, Shop & Establishment license, or GST certificate (if turnover exceeds thresholds).

Partnership

### 1. Overview

A Partnership Firm is a popular business structure where two or more individuals (partners) come together to carry out a business and share its profits and losses. Governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, this structure is based on a "Partnership Deed"—a written agreement that defines the roles, capital, and profit-sharing ratio. While registration is optional, it is highly recommended for legal standing and the ability to enforce contractual rights.

### 2. Features

*   **Minimum Partners:** Requires at least two individuals; maximum 50.
*   **Mutual Agency:** Every partner is both an agent and a principal for the firm and other partners.
*   **Partnership Deed:** The internal relationship is governed by a legally binding written agreement.
*   **Unlimited Liability:** Partners are personally and jointly liable for all debts of the firm.
*   **No Separate Legal Entity:** The firm is not a distinct entity from its partners.
*   **Profit/Loss Sharing:** Earnings and liabilities are shared as per the predetermined ratio in the deed.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Ease of Formation:** Can be started with a simple partnership deed; registration is not mandatory.
*   **Combined Resources:** Allows for greater capital and a wider range of expertise from multiple partners.
*   **Shared Burden:** Losses and responsibilities are distributed among the partners.
*   **Operational Simplicity:** Fewer regulatory filings compared to LLPs or Companies.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Personal Liability:** Unlimited risk to personal assets for business obligations.
*   **Conflict Potential:** Disputes among partners can lead to operational paralysis or dissolution.
*   **Instability:** The death, retirement, or insolvency of a partner can lead to the dissolution of the firm.
*   **Limited Capital Expansion:** Limited by the number of partners; cannot raise funds from the public.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Number of Partners:** Minimum 2 and maximum 50.
*   **Competence:** All partners must be of sound mind, 18+ years old, and not disqualified by law.
*   **Lawful Object:** The purpose of the partnership must be to carry out a legal business.
*   **Agreement:** There must be a clear intention to share profits and losses.

### 5. Documents

*   **Partnership Deed:** Drafted on non-judicial stamp paper and notarized.
*   **Partner KYC:** PAN Card, Aadhaar Card/Passport, and passport-size photos of all partners.
*   **Business Address Proof:** Rent agreement or sale deed along with a recent utility bill and NOC from the landlord.
*   **Firm PAN:** Copy of the PAN card applied for in the firm's name.
*   **Registration Application:** Form 1 signed by all partners (for registered firms).

Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)

### 1. Overview

Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is a modern corporate structure that combines the flexibility of a partnership with the limited liability of a company. Governed by the LLP Act, 2008, it is a separate legal entity from its partners, meaning the firm can own property and be held liable for its own debts. It is a preferred choice for professionals and small-to-medium businesses as it protects partners' personal assets from the firm's liabilities or the negligence of other partners.

### 2. Features

*   **Separate Legal Entity:** The LLP exists independently of its partners and has perpetual succession.
*   **Limited Liability:** A partner's liability is limited to their agreed-upon capital contribution.
*   **Minimum Two Partners:** Requires at least two partners; no upper limit on the maximum number.
*   **Designated Partners:** At least two individuals must be designated partners, with one being an Indian resident.
*   **LLP Agreement:** Governed by a mutual agreement filed with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA).
*   **No Minimum Capital:** Can be started with any amount of capital contribution.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Asset Protection:** Personal assets of partners are safe from business creditors.
*   **Perpetual Succession:** The LLP continues to exist regardless of changes in partners.
*   **Operational Flexibility:** Less complex and cheaper to maintain than a Private Limited Company.
*   **Tax Efficiency:** No Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) on profits shared with partners.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Public Disclosure:** Financial statements and annual returns are public documents.
*   **Compliance Penalties:** Heavy daily fines for late filing of mandatory MCA forms.
*   **Funding Limits:** Cannot raise capital from the public or issue equity shares.
*   **Internal Governance:** Requires consensus for major decisions as per the LLP agreement.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Partners:** Minimum 2 individuals or body corporates.
*   **Designated Partners:** Minimum 2 individuals; at least one must be a resident of India (120+ days stay).
*   **DSC & DPIN:** All designated partners must have a Digital Signature Certificate and a DPIN.
*   **Unique Name:** The name must end with "LLP" and not be similar to existing entities or trademarks.
*   **Lawful Object:** Must be formed for a legal profit-making business.

### 5. Documents

*   **Partner KYC:** Self-attested PAN, Aadhaar/Voter ID/Passport, and passport-size photos.
*   **Address Proof:** Latest utility bill or bank statement (not older than 2 months) for all partners.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Rent agreement/sale deed, recent utility bill, and NOC from the landlord.
*   **LLP Agreement:** Executed on stamp paper and filed within 30 days of incorporation.
*   **Foreign Nationals:** Apostilled/notarized copies of Passport and address proof.

One Person Company (OPC)

### 1. Overview

One Person Company (OPC) is a corporate structure introduced under the Companies Act, 2013, that allows a single individual to incorporate a company. It provides the limited liability protection and separate legal identity of a private limited company while allowing the sole owner to maintain full control. It is designed for solo entrepreneurs and freelancers who wish to formalize their business without the need for a second director or shareholder, bridging the gap between a proprietorship and a corporate entity.

### 2. Features

*   **Single Shareholder:** Owned by one natural person who is an Indian citizen and resident.
*   **Separate Legal Entity:** The company is a distinct legal person capable of owning assets and suing in its own name.
*   **Limited Liability:** The owner's personal assets are protected; liability is limited to the share capital.
*   **Nominee Requirement:** Mandatory appointment of a nominee to ensure business continuity in case of death/incapacity.
*   **Perpetual Succession:** The company continues to exist through the nominee after the original member.
*   **Board Structure:** Can have a maximum of 15 directors, but only one shareholder.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Limited Liability:** Protects the owner from personal financial ruin due to business debts.
*   **Better Credibility:** Corporate status makes it easier to secure loans and participate in government tenders.
*   **Easy Management:** Full control with no requirement for general body meetings or consensus.
*   **Perpetual Existence:** The business does not die with the owner.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **High Compliance:** Must undergo annual audits and file returns with the Registrar of Companies (ROC).
*   **Growth Caps:** Must convert to a Private Limited Company if turnover exceeds ₹2 Crore or capital exceeds ₹50 Lakh.
*   **Sole Responsibility:** The success and continuity heavily depend on a single individual's health and skills.
*   **Taxation:** Subject to high corporate tax rates compared to individual income tax slabs.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Individual:** Must be a natural person, Indian citizen, and resident (120+ days stay in India).
*   **One OPC Limit:** A person can be a member and nominee in only one OPC at a time.
*   **Non-Charitable:** Cannot be formed for Section 8 (charitable) purposes.
*   **Non-Financial:** Cannot engage in non-banking financial investment activities.
*   **Minor:** A minor cannot become a member or nominee of an OPC.

### 5. Documents

*   **Director/Nominee KYC:** PAN Card (mandatory), Aadhaar Card, and recent passport-sized photos.
*   **Address Proof:** Latest Bank Statement or Utility Bill (not older than 2 months) for both individuals.
*   **Nominee Consent:** Written consent of the nominee in Form INC-3.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Sale Deed/Rent Agreement, latest utility bill, and NOC from the owner.
*   **Constitutional Docs:** Memorandum of Association (MoA) and Articles of Association (AoA).

Private Limited Company

### 1. Overview

A Private Limited Company is the most popular corporate structure for startups and growing businesses in India. Governed by the Companies Act, 2013, it offers limited liability protection to its shareholders and maintains a separate legal identity. It allows for easy fundraising through the issuance of shares, provides high credibility with stakeholders, and is the preferred choice for venture capital and angel investors. It requires a minimum of two directors and two shareholders, ensuring a structured governance model.

### 2. Features

*   **Minimum Members:** Requires at least two shareholders and two directors.
*   **Limited Liability:** Shareholders are only liable for the unpaid value of their shares.
*   **Perpetual Succession:** The company’s existence is not affected by changes in ownership or management.
*   **Separate Entity:** Can own property, enter into contracts, and sue in its own name.
*   **Fundraising:** Can raise capital by issuing shares to private investors (up to 200 members).
*   **Resident Director:** At least one director must be an Indian resident.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Fundraising Capacity:** Highly attractive to investors due to the ability to issue equity and debt.
*   **Credibility:** Subject to rigorous reporting standards, enhancing trust with banks and global clients.
*   **Scale:** Ideal for businesses planning to expand globally or grow into large enterprises.
*   **Exit Options:** Facilitates easy transfer of shares for founders and investors.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Compliance Burden:** Strict mandatory annual filings, audits, and board meetings.
*   **Setup Costs:** Higher initial registration and professional fees compared to other structures.
*   **Lack of Privacy:** Financial statements and director details are publicly accessible on the MCA portal.
*   **Complexity:** Winding up or closing the company is a long and expensive legal process.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Directors/Members:** Minimum 2 and maximum 200 members; minimum 2 directors.
*   **Resident Director:** One director must be a resident of India (182+ days stay).
*   **DSC & DIN:** All directors must possess valid Digital Signature Certificates and DINs.
*   **Unique Name:** Must end with "Private Limited" and be distinct from existing entities.
*   **Address:** Must have a registered office address within Indian territory.

### 5. Documents

*   **Director KYC:** PAN Card, Aadhaar Card, and recent passport-size photographs.
*   **Address Proof:** Latest utility bill or bank statement (not older than 2 months) for all directors.
*   **Foreign Nationals:** Passport and address proof must be apostilled/notarized in the home country.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Proof of ownership or Rent Agreement, latest utility bill, and NOC from the landlord.
*   **MoA & AoA:** Foundational documents defining objectives and internal rules.

Public Limited Company

### 1. Overview

A Public Limited Company is a corporate structure designed for large-scale business operations that intend to raise capital from the general public. Regulated by the Companies Act, 2013, it requires a minimum of seven shareholders and three directors. Its defining feature is the ability to list shares on a stock exchange and offer them to the public through an IPO. It provides the highest level of transparency, limited liability, and credibility, making it suitable for capital-intensive industries and multi-national operations.

### 2. Features

*   **Public Fundraising:** Can invite the general public to subscribe to its shares and debentures.
*   **No Member Limit:** Requires at least 7 shareholders; no upper limit on the maximum number of members.
*   **Minimum Directors:** Must have at least 3 directors; at least one must be an Indian resident.
*   **Free Transferability:** Shares can be freely traded on the open market or stock exchanges.
*   **Regulatory Scrutiny:** Subject to strict oversight from the MCA and SEBI (if listed).
*   **Limited Liability:** Personal assets of shareholders are protected from company debts.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Capital Accumulation:** Ability to raise massive amounts of capital from the public for expansion.
*   **Brand Prestige:** Listing on a stock exchange significantly boosts reputation and brand value.
*   **Liquidity:** Provides shareholders with an easy exit through the trading of shares.
*   **Growth Potential:** Ideal for major research, development, and large-scale infrastructure projects.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **High Compliance:** Intense regulatory reporting, mandatory audits, and public financial disclosures.
*   **Loss of Control:** Founders may lose majority control as ownership is spread across thousands of shareholders.
*   **Costly Setup:** Most expensive structure to incorporate and maintain due to extensive legal requirements.
*   **Market Vulnerability:** Valuation is subject to market fluctuations and public sentiment.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Members/Directors:** Minimum 7 members and 3 directors.
*   **Resident Director:** At least one director must be an Indian resident.
*   **DSC & DIN:** All directors and subscribers must have valid Digital Signature Certificates and DINs.
*   **Unique Name:** Must end with the word "Limited" and not infringe on existing trademarks.
*   **Public Prospectus:** Must be prepared to issue a prospectus for public share subscription.

### 5. Documents

*   **Director/Subscriber KYC:** PAN Card, Aadhaar Card, and recent passport-size photos.
*   **Address Proof:** Latest utility bill or bank statement (not older than 2 months) for all directors.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Proof of ownership or Rent Agreement, latest utility bill, and NOC from the property owner.
*   **MoA & AoA:** Customized foundational documents for a public-facing corporate entity.
*   **Prospectus:** Detailed document for public share offering (if applicable).

Producer Company

### 1. Overview

A Producer Company is a unique hybrid of a private limited company and a cooperative society, specifically designed for primary producers like farmers, artisans, and small-scale manufacturers. Governed by the Companies Act, 2013, it allows producers to organize collectively to improve their bargaining power, access credit, and manage the production-to-market value chain. It combines the benefits of limited liability with the democratic principle of "one member, one vote," ensuring that members maintain control over their enterprise.

### 2. Features

*   **Producer Membership:** Formed by at least 10 primary producers or 2 producer institutions.
*   **Democratic Governance:** Each member has one vote, regardless of the number of shares held.
*   **Limited Liability:** Members' personal assets are protected; liability is limited to share capital.
*   **Dual Status:** Operates as a Private Limited Company but follows cooperative principles.
*   **Perpetual Succession:** The company continues to exist independent of its members.
*   **Board Structure:** Minimum of 5 and maximum of 15 directors, all of whom must be producers.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Bargaining Power:** Collective procurement of inputs and marketing of produce reduces costs and increases income.
*   **Tax Benefits:** Income from core agricultural activities is often exempt from income tax under Section 10(1).
*   **Access to Credit:** Easier to secure institutional loans from NABARD and banks than individual farmers.
*   **Professional Management:** Allows producers to hire experts to manage finance, marketing, and logistics.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Compliance Burden:** Must follow the same strict regulatory and audit norms as other companies.
*   **Internal Conflicts:** Large memberships can lead to disputes over management and profit distribution.
*   **Capital Limits:** Cannot raise funds from the general public; capital is limited to member contributions.
*   **Management Skills:** Often suffers from a lack of professional corporate expertise among the producer members.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Founders:** Minimum 10 primary producers or 2 producer institutions.
*   **Directors:** Minimum 5 directors; all must be primary producers.
*   **Primary Producer Proof:** Members must provide evidence of being producers (e.g., land records or local certificates).
*   **DSC & DIN:** All directors must have valid Digital Signature Certificates and DINs.
*   **Unique Name:** Must end with the words "Producer Limited Company."

### 5. Documents

*   **Member/Director KYC:** PAN Card, Aadhaar Card, and recent passport-size photos.
*   **Address Proof:** Latest utility bill or bank statement (not older than 2 months) for all members.
*   **Producer Proof:** Self-declaration or certificate from local authorities confirming producer status.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Proof of ownership or Rent Agreement, latest utility bill, and NOC from the owner.
*   **MoA & AoA:** Standardized foundational documents as per the Companies Act for Producer entities.

Nidhi Company Registration

### 1. Overview

A Nidhi Company is a type of Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) governed by Section 406 of the Companies Act, 2013 and the Nidhi Rules, 2014. Its primary objective is to cultivate the habit of thrift and savings among its members and to facilitate member-only deposits and lending. Operating on the principle of mutuality, Nidhi Companies are primarily regulated by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) and are generally exempt from the core provisions of the RBI Act, making them an accessible model for small-scale community banking and savings.

### 2. Features

*   **Mutuality Principle:** Operates exclusively for the benefit of its members; it can only borrow from and lend to its registered shareholders.
*   **MCA Regulation:** Governed by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; unlike other NBFCs, it does not require a prior license from the RBI to commence operations.
*   **Corporate Structure:** Must be incorporated as a Public Limited Company with a minimum paid-up equity share capital of ₹10 lakhs.
*   **Naming Convention:** The name of the company must mandatory end with the words "Nidhi Limited".
*   **Restricted Instruments:** Prohibited from issuing preference shares, debentures, or any other debt instruments.
*   **No Public Solicitation:** Cannot advertise or solicit deposits from the general public or non-members.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Simplified Regulatory Framework:** No requirement for a stringent RBI license or high capital adequacy norms compared to traditional NBFCs.
*   **Promotes Savings:** Encourages financial discipline and thrift among members in local or rural communities.
*   **Low Operational Risk:** Lending is restricted to members, often secured by collateral like gold or property, ensuring higher peer accountability.
*   **Member Control:** Management and financial activities remain within a closed group, ensuring operational autonomy.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Restricted Funding Pool:** Capital is limited to deposits from members only, hindering large-scale expansion.
*   **Limited Business Scope:** Prohibited from engaging in chit funds, hire purchase, leasing, or insurance businesses.
*   **Lack of Deposit Insurance:** Deposits are not covered by the DICGC, exposing members to higher risk in case of mismanagement.
*   **Strict Membership Requirements:** Must reach a minimum of 200 members and maintain a specific Net Owned Fund (NOF) to deposit ratio (1:20) within the first year.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Minimum Members/Directors:** Requires a minimum of 7 members and 3 directors at the time of incorporation.
*   **Minimum Capital:** Minimum paid-up equity share capital of ₹10 lakhs (updated to ₹20 lakhs for NOF compliance within one year).
*   **Post-Incorporation Mandate:** Must achieve at least 200 members and a Net Owned Fund (NOF) of ₹20 lakhs within one year of registration.
*   **Unencumbered Deposits:** Must maintain unencumbered term deposits of not less than 10% of its outstanding deposits.

### 5. Documents

*   **Director/Subscriber ID:** PAN Card (mandatory for Indians) and Aadhaar/Voter ID/Passport.
*   **Address Proof:** Latest Bank Statement or Utility Bill (not older than 2 months) for all directors and members.
*   **Photographs:** Recent passport-sized photographs of all proposed directors.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Sale Deed/Rent Agreement along with the latest utility bill and a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the owner.
*   **Constitutional Documents:** Draft Memorandum of Association (MoA) and Articles of Association (AoA) prepared as per Nidhi Rules.

Section 8 / NGO Company Registration

### 1. Overview

A Section 8 Company is a specialized legal entity formed under the Companies Act, 2013, for charitable or non-profit purposes. It is established to promote commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, and environmental protection. Unlike regular companies, a Section 8 Company is prohibited from distributing profits or dividends to its members; all income must be reinvested to further its charitable mission. This structure combines the credibility of a corporate entity with the tax benefits of a non-profit, making it the preferred choice for large-scale NGOs and social enterprises.

### 2. Features

*   **Non-Profit Mandate:** All profits and income must be used exclusively for the promotion of the company’s charitable objects.
*   **No Dividend Distribution:** Strictly prohibited from paying any dividend or distributing surplus to its members.
*   **Government License:** Requires a special license from the Central Government (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) to operate as a non-profit.
*   **Limited Liability:** Protects the personal assets of members/directors, as their liability is limited to their contribution.
*   **Waiver of Suffix:** Exempted from using "Private Limited" or "Limited" in its name, allowing suffixes like "Foundation," "Association," or "Council."
*   **Separate Legal Entity:** Can own property, enter into contracts, and sue/be sued in its own name, independent of its members.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Tax Exemptions:** Eligible for significant tax benefits under Sections 12A/12AB and 80G of the Income Tax Act, incentivizing donors.
*   **High Credibility:** Subjects the organization to rigorous corporate reporting and audit standards, enhancing trust with stakeholders.
*   **CSR Funding Access:** Preferred vehicle for receiving Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) funds from for-profit corporations.
*   **Zero Stamp Duty:** Many states exempt Section 8 companies from paying stamp duty on registration documents.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Stringent Compliances:** Must adhere to complex regulatory requirements of the Companies Act, including mandatory annual audits and filings.
*   **No Profit Withdrawal:** Founders and members cannot draw profits; financial interest is limited to reasonable remuneration for services.
*   **Objective Restrictions:** Any change in the company's Memorandum of Association (MoA) requires prior approval from the Central Government.
*   **Winding Up Restrictions:** Upon dissolution, remaining assets must be transferred to another Section 8 company with similar objects.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Applicants:** Individuals, associations of persons, or existing companies (including foreign entities) can apply.
*   **Structure:** Minimum 2 directors/members for a Private Section 8 and 3 directors/7 members for a Public Section 8.
*   **Resident Director:** At least one director must be a resident of India (stayed 182+ days in the previous year).
*   **Charitable Object:** The primary goal must be the promotion of social welfare, education, charity, or other specified non-profit objects.
*   **No Minimum Capital:** No requirement for a minimum paid-up share capital.

### 5. Documents

*   **Director/Subscriber KYC:** Self-attested PAN (mandatory), Aadhaar/Voter ID, and recent passport-sized photos.
*   **Address Proof:** Latest Bank Statement or Utility Bill (not older than 2 months) for all directors.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Proof of ownership (Sale Deed) or rental agreement, along with a recent utility bill and NOC from the landlord.
*   **MoA/AoA (INC-13):** Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association drafted specifically for non-profit objects.
*   **Financial Projections:** An estimate of future income and expenditure for the next three years.
*   **Declarations:** Forms INC-14 (Professional Declaration) and INC-15 (Applicant Declaration).

Trust Registration

### 1. Overview

Trust Registration in India is the legal process of establishing a Trust, an arrangement where a "Settlor" (owner) transfers assets to "Trustees" to be managed for the benefit of "Beneficiaries." While Private Trusts (for family/individuals) are governed by the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, Public and Charitable Trusts are governed by state-specific Public Trust Acts (e.g., Bombay Public Trusts Act) and relevant provisions of the Income Tax Act. A Trust is a popular vehicle for charitable activities due to its operational simplicity and the ability to hold property for long-term social objectives.

### 2. Features

*   **Three Pillars:** Comprises a Settlor (creator), Trustees (managers), and Beneficiaries (recipients of benefits).
*   **Trust Deed:** The foundation document that outlines the objectives, rules, and powers of the trustees.
*   **Irrevocable Nature:** Most public charitable trusts are irrevocable; once the property is settled, it cannot be taken back by the settlor.
*   **Perpetual Succession:** A Trust continues to exist even if trustees change, as long as the trust property and objectives remain.
*   **Registration Authority:** Usually registered with the Sub-Registrar (Private) or Charity Commissioner (Public) of the respective state.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Operational Ease:** Generally involves fewer compliance hurdles and meetings compared to a Section 8 Company or Society.
*   **Long-term Stability:** Ideal for managing large endowments, ancestral properties, or family legacies across generations.
*   **Tax Benefits:** Public trusts can apply for 12A/80G status to gain income tax exemptions and attract tax-deductible donations.
*   **Control:** The settlor has significant initial control over defining the rules and selecting the first board of trustees.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Limited Regulatory Flexibility:** Once registered, the core objects of the trust deed are difficult to amend without court intervention.
*   **State-Specific Laws:** Lack of a single central law for public trusts leads to varied procedures and costs across different states.
*   **Transparency Concerns:** Often perceived as less transparent than Section 8 companies, which may affect large-scale institutional funding.
*   **Trustee Liability:** Trustees can be held personally liable for breaches of trust or negligence in managing the corpus.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Settlor:** Any person (individual, company, or association) capable of entering into a contract.
*   **Trustees:** Minimum 2 trustees (no upper limit unless specified in the deed); must be mentally sound and not disqualified by law.
*   **Beneficiaries:** Must be clearly identifiable individuals (Private) or a section of the public (Public/Charitable).
*   **Lawful Object:** The purpose must not be illegal, immoral, or against public policy.
*   **Property:** Must have a specific "corpus" (movable or immovable property) to be settled upon the trust.

### 5. Documents

*   **Trust Deed:** Stamped and notarized document on jurisdictional stamp paper.
*   **Settlor/Trustee IDs:** Self-attested PAN and Aadhaar/Voter ID/Passport for all parties.
*   **Photographs:** Two passport-sized photos of the settlor and all trustees.
*   **Address Proof (Individuals):** Latest Bank Statement or Utility Bill for all trustees.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Proof of ownership (Sale Deed) or Rent Agreement, with a recent utility bill and NOC from the property owner.
*   **Witness IDs:** ID and address proofs for at least two witnesses who must be present during registration.

Society Registration

### 1. Overview

Society Registration is the legal process of forming a non-profit entity under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 (or relevant state-specific versions). A Society is established when a group of at least seven individuals unite for a common social purpose, such as the promotion of science, literature, fine arts, or charitable activities. Societies are democratic in nature, governed by an elected Managing Committee, and are suitable for community-based clubs, educational institutions, and social welfare associations.

### 2. Features

*   **Minimum Members:** Requires at least seven members (can include foreigners and other societies) to form the founding body.
*   **Democratic Governance:** Managed by an elected Governing Body or Managing Committee, with regular elections as per the bylaws.
*   **Memorandum of Association (MoA):** The core document defining the society's name, objectives, and the names of the founding members.
*   **Rules & Regulations:** A separate document outlining the internal bylaws, membership rules, and meeting procedures.
*   **State Control:** Registered and regulated by the Registrar of Societies of the respective state government.
*   **Separate Legal Identity:** Once registered, the society can hold property, open bank accounts, and enter into legal contracts in its own name.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Low Cost of Formation:** Generally the most affordable non-profit structure to register in terms of government fees.
*   **Tax Benefits:** Eligible for income tax exemptions (12A/80G) if the objectives are purely charitable or religious.
*   **Democratic Setup:** Ensures that control is not centralized; members have equal voting rights in the general body.
*   **Limited Liability:** The personal assets of members are protected from the society's liabilities, provided they act within the bylaws.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Compliance Complexity:** Requires regular filing of annual lists of the managing committee and audited financial statements with the Registrar.
*   **Public Scrutiny:** The records and filings of a society are public documents, subject to inspection by any citizen.
*   **Internal Conflicts:** Democratic structures are prone to factionalism and disputes among members, which can lead to legal litigation.
*   **State-Specific Variations:** Procedures, membership requirements, and renewal rules vary significantly across different states in India.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Number of Members:** Minimum 7 members are mandatory (10 in some states like Delhi for certain types).
*   **Type of Members:** Can include individuals, companies, other societies, and foreign nationals.
*   **Lawful Object:** Must be formed for purposes specified in Section 20 of the Act (e.g., charity, science, art, education).
*   **Founding Body:** Must have a designated President, Secretary, and Treasurer among the founding members.
*   **Geographical Limitation:** Usually restricted to operating within the state where it is registered.

### 5. Documents

*   **Memorandum of Association (MoA):** Signed by all founding members and witnessed by a professional.
*   **Rules & Regulations:** Certified copy of the bylaws signed by at least three members of the governing body.
*   **Member IDs:** Self-attested PAN and Aadhaar/Voter ID for all founding members.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Proof of ownership (Sale Deed) or Rent Agreement, along with the latest utility bill and NOC from the owner.
*   **Meeting Minutes:** A copy of the minutes of the first meeting where the decision to form the society was taken.
*   **Affidavit:** A declaration stating the society’s name is not identical to any existing entity.

Foreign Subsidiary / Indian Subsidiary Registration

### 1. Overview

An Indian Subsidiary (often referred to as a Foreign Subsidiary when the parent is outside India) is a Private Limited Company incorporated in India where more than 50% of the share capital is held by a foreign corporate body. It is the most preferred route for multinational companies to establish a permanent presence in India, providing a separate legal identity and the ability to conduct full-scale commercial, manufacturing, or service activities. Unlike a Branch or Liaison Office, a subsidiary is treated as a domestic Indian company for all regulatory purposes, enabling it to access the vast Indian market with limited liability for the parent entity.

### 2. Features

*   **Separate Legal Entity:** The subsidiary is a distinct legal person from its parent company, capable of owning assets and entering into contracts in its own name.
*   **Ownership Structure:** At least 50.1% (up to 100% for Wholly Owned Subsidiaries) of shares are held by the foreign holding company.
*   **Management:** Requires a minimum of two directors, at least one of whom must be an Indian Resident (stayed in India for 182+ days in the previous year).
*   **FDI Compliance:** Governed by the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) policy of India, allowing 100% ownership under the "Automatic Route" in most sectors.
*   **Operational Freedom:** Can engage in any lawful business activity, including trading, manufacturing, and E-commerce.
*   **Repatriation:** Profits can be repatriated to the foreign parent through dividends, subject to applicable taxes and FEMA regulations.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Limited Liability:** The parent company's liability is limited to its capital contribution, protecting its global assets from the subsidiary's debts.
*   **Market Access:** Provides direct access to India's huge consumer base and local government tenders.
*   **Talent Acquisition:** Ability to hire local skilled professionals and offer ESOPs under Indian law.
*   **Tax Benefits:** Eligible for various manufacturing incentives (like PLI schemes) and benefits under Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA).
*   **Local Financing:** Can raise debt from Indian banks and access local capital markets.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Complex Setup:** Involves notarization and apostille of foreign documents, making the incorporation process longer than for domestic companies.
*   **High Compliance Burden:** Requires strict adherence to the Companies Act, Income Tax, GST, and FEMA (RBI) reporting (FC-GPR).
*   **Transfer Pricing:** Transactions with the parent company must follow "Arm’s Length" pricing, requiring detailed documentation and audits.
*   **Closure Hurdles:** Winding up a subsidiary is a lengthy and expensive legal process compared to other entry routes.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Shareholders:** Minimum 2 shareholders (one can be the foreign parent company and another its nominee).
*   **Directors:** Minimum 2 directors; at least one must be an Indian resident.
*   **Parent Company:** Must be a legally incorporated entity in its home country.
*   **Business Objects:** The activity must be permitted under the current FDI policy (Automatic or Approval route).
*   **Office:** Must have a physical registered office address in India.

### 5. Documents

*   **Foreign Parent Docs:** Certificate of Incorporation, MoA, AoA, and a Board Resolution authorizing the Indian investment (all must be Apostilled/Consularised).
*   **Foreign Director IDs:** Apostilled/Consularised copy of Passport and latest Utility Bill/Bank Statement (not older than 2 months).
*   **Indian Director IDs:** Self-attested PAN, Aadhaar, and latest Utility Bill/Bank Statement.
*   **Registered Office Proof:** Proof of ownership or Rent Agreement, along with a recent utility bill and NOC from the owner.
*   **FDI Reporting:** Documents for filing Form FC-GPR with the RBI after capital infusion.

Microfinance Company Registration

### 1. Overview

A Microfinance Company (MFC) is a financial institution that provides small-scale credit and other financial services to low-income individuals or micro-entrepreneurs who lack access to traditional banking. In India, microfinance can be conducted through two primary legal structures: the **NBFC-MFI** (Non-Banking Financial Company-Micro Finance Institution), which is a for-profit entity regulated by the RBI, and the **Section 8 Microfinance Company**, which is a non-profit entity regulated by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA). While NBFC-MFIs can scale significantly and access commercial funding, Section 8 companies are preferred for community-focused, smaller-scale social initiatives.

### 2. Features

*   **Financial Inclusion:** Specifically targets the "unbanked" population in rural and semi-urban areas.
*   **Collateral-Free Loans:** Provides micro-loans (usually up to ₹1.25 lakh) without requiring physical security, often using the Joint Liability Group (JLG) model.
*   **NBFC-MFI (RBI Route):** Requires a massive minimum capital of ₹10 Crore (₹2 Crore in North East) and formal RBI licensing.
*   **Section 8 (MCA Route):** No minimum capital requirement and does not require a prior RBI license to start lending from own funds/grants.
*   **Dual Mission:** Balances social impact (poverty alleviation) with institutional sustainability.
*   **Interest Rate Caps:** Subject to RBI's "Fair Practices Code" and pricing regulations to prevent predatory lending.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Social Impact:** Directly empowers women and marginalized communities by providing capital for income-generating activities.
*   **High Repayment Rates:** The group lending model ensures high accountability and low NPA (Non-Performing Asset) rates.
*   **Alternative Credit:** Protects the poor from exploitative local moneylenders.
*   **Scalability (NBFC):** Ability to raise large-scale capital from banks and domestic/international markets.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Regulatory Rigidity:** NBFC-MFIs must strictly follow RBI's capital and lending norms.
*   **High Setup Costs:** The ₹10 Crore capital requirement for NBFCs is a major barrier for new entrants.
*   **Interest Rate Caps:** Limits the potential profit margins compared to other high-risk lending.
*   **Borrower Over-indebtedness Risk:** Requires strict credit checks to ensure borrowers can repay.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Structure:** Must be a Private Limited, Public Limited, or Section 8 Company.
*   **Capital (NBFC-MFI):** Mandatory Net Owned Fund (NOF) of ₹10 Crore (Proof of "No Lien" required).
*   **Management:** Directors must have professional experience in finance, banking, or social sectors; at least 1/3rd must be independent (for NBFCs).
*   **Clean Record:** Promoters and directors must satisfy "Fit and Proper" criteria (no criminal record or financial defaults).
*   **Lending Ratio:** For NBFC-MFIs, at least 85% of net assets must be "Qualifying Assets" (micro-loans to low-income groups).

### 5. Documents

*   **Incorporation Docs:** Certificate of Incorporation, MoA, and AoA specifically stating the microfinance objective.
*   **Capital Proof:** Bank certificate showing deposit of the required NOF (for NBFC-MFI).
*   **KYC of Promoters:** PAN, Aadhaar, and detailed net worth certificates for all directors/shareholders.
*   **Business Plan:** A detailed 3-year plan covering lending methodology, credit assessment, and financial projections.
*   **RBI Application:** Detailed Form for Certificate of Registration (CoR) along with supporting annexures.
*   **Statutory Auditor Report:** Certificate from a Chartered Accountant regarding the Net Owned Fund.

Startup India Registration

### 1. Overview

Startup India is a flagship initiative of the Government of India, launched to build a strong ecosystem that is conducive to the growth of startup businesses. To access the various fiscal and regulatory benefits, an entity must be recognized by the **DPIIT** (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade). Recognition is open to Private Limited Companies, LLPs, and registered Partnership Firms that are less than 10 years old and have an annual turnover not exceeding ₹100 Crore. The core requirement is that the business must be working towards innovation, development, or improvement of products or processes, or have a scalable business model with high potential for employment generation.

### 2. Features

*   **DPIIT Recognition:** Provides official "Startup" status, unlocking various government schemes.
*   **Self-Certification:** Startups can self-certify compliance for 6 labor laws and 3 environmental laws for a period of 3 to 5 years.
*   **Income Tax Exemption:** Eligible startups can apply for a 3-year tax holiday (under Section 80-IAC) within their first 10 years.
*   **IPR Benefits:** Fast-track patent applications and up to 80% rebate on patent filing fees and 50% on trademark fees.
*   **Easier Procurement:** Relaxations in "prior turnover" and "prior experience" criteria for government tenders.
*   **SIDBI Fund of Funds:** Access to capital through government-backed venture funds.

### 3. Advantages & Disadvantages

**Advantages:**
*   **Tax Savings:** Significant reduction in tax liability, allowing reinvestment of profits into growth.
*   **Credibility:** The DPIIT certificate is a "seal of approval" for investors, banks, and corporate partners.
*   **Winding Up:** Faster exit/winding-up process (within 90 days) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code.
*   **Networking:** Access to the Startup India Hub, mentorship programs, and government-sponsored incubators.

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Innovation Burden:** The entity must prove it is "innovative" or "scalable"; simple trading or service businesses may be rejected.
*   **Complexity of Tax Exemption:** While DPIIT recognition is easy, getting the Section 80-IAC tax exemption certificate involves a separate, much tougher evaluation.
*   **Age Limit:** Benefits cease once the company completes 10 years from incorporation.
*   **Turnover Cap:** Reaching ₹100 Crore in annual turnover results in the loss of startup status.

### 4. Eligibility

*   **Entity Type:** Must be a Private Limited Company, LLP, or Registered Partnership. (Sole Proprietorships are ineligible).
*   **Age of Entity:** Period of existence and operations should not exceed 10 years from the date of incorporation.
*   **Annual Turnover:** Should not have exceeded ₹100 Crore in any financial year since incorporation.
*   **Innovation/Scalability:** Must work towards innovation, development, or improvement of products/services, or be a scalable business model with high potential for wealth and job creation.
*   **Originality:** The entity should not have been formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business.

### 5. Documents

*   **Certificate of Incorporation:** Mandatory for all entities.
*   **PAN Card:** Permanent Account Number of the entity.
*   **Proof of Concept:** A brief write-up (pitch deck or video) explaining the innovative nature of the product/service.
*   **Business Details:** Description of the business activities and how it intends to generate employment or wealth.
*   **Website/App Link:** (Optional but highly recommended) To showcase the product/service to the evaluators.
*   **Awards/Patents:** (If any) Documentation of any prior recognition or intellectual property.
