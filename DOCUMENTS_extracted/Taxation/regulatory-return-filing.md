PF Return Filling

OVERVIEW

A Provident Fund (PF) is a government-managed social security system designed to provide financial stability after retirement. It functions as a compulsory, interest-bearing savings tool where a portion of your monthly income is set aside. Over your working years, these contributions—along with accrued interest—accumulate into a substantial lump sum.

For salaried employees, the Employee Provident Fund (EPF) involves mandatory contributions from both you and your employer. Conversely, the Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a voluntary scheme open to all citizens, including the self-employed. In 2026, both offer high safety and significant tax benefits, helping you build a risk-free retirement corpus.

Types of Provident Funds

There are three main variants depending on your employment status:

EPF (Employee Provident Fund): Mandatory for salaried employees in the private sector (for firms with 20+ employees).

PPF (Public Provident Fund): A voluntary scheme open to all Indian citizens (salaried, self-employed, or unemployed). It has a 15-year tenure.

GPF (General Provident Fund): Specifically for government employees.

PF Contribution Breakdown

For the Assessment Year 2026-27, the Provident Fund (PF) contribution remains a standard 12% of your Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance (DA). However, the employer's side is more complex as it is split into various social security buckets.

Here is the exact breakdown of how every rupee of your PF contribution is distributed:

The Monthly Split (Standard Case)


| Component | Contribution Rate | Contributed By | Destination Account |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employee Share | 12% | Employee | Your EPF Account |
| Employer Share | 3.67% | Employer | Your EPF Account |
| Pension Fund | 8.33% | Employer | EPS (Pension) Account |
| Insurance | 0.50% | Employer | EDLI (Life Insurance) |
| Admin Charges | 0.50% | Employer | EPFO Admin (Misc) |


The "Wage Ceiling" Logic

If your (Basic + DA) is above ₹15,000, the math changes slightly because the Pension contribution is usually capped.

Pension Cap: The employer's 8.33% contribution to the Pension Scheme (EPS) is capped at a salary of ₹15,000. This means the maximum pension deposit is generally ₹1,250 per month.

Excess to EPF: Any employer contribution amount exceeding that ₹1,250 cap is diverted back into your main EPF account, helping your lump sum grow faster.

Benefits of PF Return Filling

PF Return Filing is the digital process of submitting a detailed monthly report (ECR) to the EPFO, documenting the contributions made by both the employer and the employee.

For the Assessment Year 2026-27, timely filing has become more critical due to the "EPFO 3.0" initiative, which focuses on automated claim settlements and real-time interest credits.

Benefits for the Employee

Timely filing by the employer is the backbone of an employee's financial security.

Continuous Interest Earning: Interest is calculated on the monthly running balance. If your employer delays filing, you lose out on the compounding effect for that period.

Instant Claim Settlement (EPFO 3.0): In 2026, 95% of advance claims (for marriage, medical, etc.) are settled via Auto-Mode. This is only possible if the monthly returns are updated; otherwise, the system rejects the claim.

Accurate Passbook: It ensures your passbook is always up-to-date, allowing you to use it as a valid financial document for home loans or visa applications.

Insurance Protection (EDLI): Timely filing keeps the Employee Deposit Linked Insurance active. In the event of an unfortunate incident, the nominee can only claim the life insurance (up to ₹7 Lakh) if the service records are compliant.

Benefits for the Employer

Beyond avoiding legal trouble, filing returns serves as a significant business advantage:

Avoidance of "Damages" & Interest: Late payments attract 12% p.a. interest (u/s 7Q) and penal damages ranging from 5% to 25% (u/s 14B). Automated system-based calculations in 2026 make these penalties unavoidable for defaulters.

Budget 2026 Tax Deduction: Under the latest Finance Bill 2026, employers can now claim the employee's PF contribution as a business expense even if there is a slight delay, provided it is deposited before the ITR filing deadline. This is a major relief from the previous "strict timeline" rule.

Improved Retention & Trust: Transparency in social security contributions is a key metric for "Great Place to Work" certifications and building a credible corporate image.

Simplified Audits: Regular filing ensures that Statutory and Tax Audits proceed smoothly without discrepancies in the "Provident Fund Payable" ledgers.

Eligible Criteria for PF Return Filling

To file PF (Provident Fund) Returns for the Assessment Year 2026-27, the eligibility criteria apply primarily to the employer, as it is their statutory responsibility to report and deposit contributions for their eligible workforce.

The criteria are divided into three categories: Mandatory, Voluntary, and Employee-Specific.

Mandatory Criteria for Establishments

An establishment is legally required to register with the EPFO and file monthly returns (ECR) if:

Employee Count: It employs 20 or more persons. This count includes permanent, temporary, part-time, and contract workers.

Continuity Rule: Once a company is registered under the EPF Act, it must continue filing returns even if the employee count falls below 20 at a later date.

Industry Specifics: Certain notified industries (like beedi, brick, or jute) may be required to register even with a smaller workforce.

Voluntary Criteria (For Small Businesses)

Even if you do not meet the 20-employee threshold, you are eligible to file PF returns if:

Mutual Consent: The employer and a majority of the employees agree to opt for the scheme voluntarily to provide social security benefits.

Startup Growth: Many startups register voluntarily with fewer than 10 employees to build institutional credibility.

Note: Once you register voluntarily, all compliance rules (monthly filing, deadlines, and penalties) apply exactly as they do for mandatory establishments.

Eligibility Criteria for Employees

While the employer files the return, they can only include employees who meet the following criteria:

Wage Ceiling: It is mandatory for employees earning a Basic + DA of ₹15,000 or less per month.

2026 Update: The government is currently finalizing a proposal to increase this limit to ₹25,000.

Excluded Employees: Employees earning more than the wage ceiling (e.g., ₹50,000) are technically "excluded," but they can still be included in the return if both the employer and employee give written consent (Section 26(6)).

UAN & KYC: To be included in a 2026 return, an employee must have a Universal Account Number (UAN) that is Aadhaar-seeded and KYC-verified. The portal now rejects ECR entries for non-verified members.

ESIC Return Filling

OVERVIEW

ESIC Return Filing is the mandatory online process where employers submit a comprehensive report of contributions to the Employees' State Insurance Corporation. This filing documents the monthly 4% contribution (3.25% from the employer and 0.75% from the employee) deducted from the gross wages of eligible workers.

In 2026, returns are filed bi-annually: the April–September period is due by November 11th, and the October–March period by May 12th. Timely filing ensures that employees maintain uninterrupted access to medical, maternity, and disability benefits while shielding employers from steep interest penalties and legal scrutiny.

Benefits of ESIC Return Filling

ESIC Return Filing is not just a paperwork requirement; it is the mechanism that activates life-saving benefits for your employees and legal immunity for your business.

In 2026, the importance of timely filing has increased due to automated links between the ESIC portal and healthcare eligibility.

Benefits for the Employer

Filing returns is your primary defense against legal and financial liabilities.

Legal Immunity: Once an employee is registered and returns are filed, you are protected from the Workmen’s Compensation Act. You are no longer personally liable for medical costs or compensation if an employee is injured on the job.

Avoidance of Penalties: Timely filing saves you from a 12% p.a. interest charge and penal damages that can go up to 25% of the contribution amount.

Tax Deductions: Under Budget 2026, ESI contributions are only tax-deductible for the employer if they are deposited by the 15th of the month. Late payments can be "disallowed," increasing your taxable income.

Operational Smoothness: Regular filing keeps your establishment in the "Green Zone," reducing the likelihood of surprise inspections or audits by ESIC authorities.

Benefits for the Employee

For the worker, your filing is the "switch" that keeps their insurance active.

Full Medical Care: Your filing ensures the employee and their family have access to over 150+ ESIC hospitals for everything from OPD visits to major surgeries.

Cash Benefits: It validates their eligibility for Sickness Benefit (70% of wages during illness) and Maternity Benefit (100% of wages for 26 weeks).

Unemployment Cover: If an employee is laid off, they can claim up to 50% of their average salary for 90 days, but only if their past return records are clean.

Dependents’ Security: In the tragic event of an employment-related death, the employee's family receives a lifetime monthly pension, provided the returns were filed correctly.

Eligible Criteria for ESIC Return Filling

To file ESIC Returns in 2026, your business must meet specific eligibility criteria defined by the ESI Act. Compliance is handled monthly (for payments) and half-yearly (for returns).

The eligibility is split into two parts: Establishment Eligibility (who must register) and Employee Eligibility (who must be included in the return).

Establishment Eligibility (Employer)

You are eligible and mandated to register and file returns if your business meets these thresholds:

Employee Count: Generally, any establishment with 10 or more employees must register.

Note: In some states (e.g., Maharashtra, Chandigarh, and Uttar Pradesh), this threshold may still be 20 employees, though many states have moved to a uniform 10-employee rule under 2025 reforms.

Sector Type: Factories, shops, hotels, restaurants, cinemas, newspaper establishments, and private educational or medical institutions are all covered.

Auto-Registration: Businesses incorporated after February 23, 2020, through the MCA portal (SPICe+) are often auto-registered for ESIC.

Employee Eligibility (Who to include in the Filing)

An employee is eligible to be covered under your ESI return if they meet the following:

Wage Ceiling: Their gross monthly salary is ₹21,000 or less.

Persons with Disabilities (PWD): The wage ceiling is higher, at ₹25,000 per month.

Budget 2026 Alert: The government is currently reviewing a proposal to hike this limit to ₹30,000, but as of February 2026, the ₹21,000/₹25,000 limits remain the standard.

Aadhaar Seeding: In 2026, it is mandatory to seed the Aadhaar of the "Insured Person" (IP) and their dependents to enable seamless return filing and hospital access.

Daily Wage Exemption: If an employee earns an average daily wage of ₹176 or less, they are "exempt" from their contribution (0.75%), but you (the employer) must still include them in the return and pay your 3.25% share.

Filing "Nil Returns"

If your business is registered under the ESI Act but you have zero eligible employees for a particular month (e.g., all employees earn above ₹21,000), you are still mandatorily eligible and required to file a "NIL Return."

ESIC Contribution Breakdown

In 2026, the ESIC (Employees' State Insurance Corporation) contribution remains a combined 4% of an employee's gross wages. However, with the full implementation of the New Labour Codes, the "wage base" used for this calculation has been standardized to ensure fair coverage.

The 4% Contribution Split

The contribution is divided between the employer and the employee as follows:


| Contributor | Share | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employee Share | 0.75% | Deducted from the employee's monthly gross wages. |
| Employer Share | 3.25% | Paid by the company as an additional cost (not from the employee's pay). |

New "Wage Definition" (2026 Rules)

Under the Code on Social Security, the definition of "Wages" has shifted to prevent companies from artificially lowering basic pay to save on ESI costs.

The 50% Rule: Your Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance (DA) must now comprise at least 50% of the total CTC.

Impact: If your allowances (HRA, Travel, etc.) exceed 50% of your total pay, the excess amount is added back to your "Wages" for ESI calculation. This ensures more employees fall under the safety net.

Eligibility & Exemptions

Standard Wage Limit: Mandatory for employees earning a gross salary of ₹21,000 or less per month.

Disability Limit: For persons with disabilities, the limit is ₹25,000 per month.

Low Wage Exemption: Employees earning a daily average wage of ₹176 or less are exempt from their 0.75% share. The employer, however, must still contribute the full 3.25% share for them.

TDS Return Filing

OVERVIEW

TDS Return Filing is a mandatory quarterly statement submitted to the Income Tax Department by any person or business that has deducted tax while making payments. It serves as a detailed record linking the tax deposited with the specific PAN of the recipient, ensuring they receive credit for the tax paid on their behalf.

For AY 2026-27, these returns (Forms 24Q, 26Q, etc.) must be filed by the end of the month following each quarter. Timely filing is essential; it updates the recipient's Form 26AS/AIS, allowing them to claim tax credits or refunds while shielding the deductor from late fees of ₹200 per day.

What is the TDS ?

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is a "pay-as-you-earn" mechanism used by the Indian government to collect income tax at the origin of a transaction. The payer (deductor) subtracts a specific percentage before making payments like salary, rent, or interest, remitting it directly to the government on the recipient's (deductee) behalf. This ensures steady revenue collection, prevents tax evasion, and simplifies year-end tax liabilities.

Purpose of TDS Return Filing

The purpose of TDS Return Filing is to reconcile the tax deducted by a business with the specific individuals or vendors who earned the income. While the tax is deposited monthly, the Quarterly Return is the actual document that "assigns" that money to the correct person.

In 2026, the purpose has become even more critical due to the New Income Tax Act 2025 reforms, which emphasize real-time data matching.

For the Recipient (Deductee)

Tax Credit Visibility: The primary purpose is to update the recipient's Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS). Until the return is filed, the tax deducted from their pay is just "money in limbo" and doesn't show up as a credit in their account.

Claiming Refunds: If more tax was deducted than necessary (e.g., an employee has high investments but the employer deducted standard TDS), the employee can only claim a refund if the TDS return was filed accurately.

Loan & Visa Processing: TDS certificates (Form 16/16A), which are generated only after a return is filed, serve as official proof of income for banks and embassies.

For the Business (Deductor)

Statutory Compliance: It is a legal mandate. Filing proves to the government that you have fulfilled your duty as a "tax collector."

Expense Validation: Under Section 40(a)(ia), if you fail to file TDS returns or pay TDS, the government can disallow 30% of your expenses. This means you end up paying higher corporate tax because your costs aren't recognized.

Avoiding "Defaults": The portal automatically checks for "Short Deductions" or "PAN Errors." Filing returns allows you to identify and fix these errors early before they turn into heavy penalties.

For the Government

Tracking Income at Source: It allows the Income Tax Department to monitor high-value transactions in real-time. It’s harder for people to hide income when a payer has already reported it.

Steady Revenue Stream: It ensures the government doesn't have to wait until the end of the financial year to collect taxes; it receives a constant flow of funds every quarter.

Widening the Tax Base: It brings "non-filers" into the system. When a bank or client reports TDS against a PAN, the department knows exactly who is earning above the threshold.

Benefits of TDS Return Filing

Filing TDS Returns is a critical compliance activity that benefits both the person paying (the deductor) and the person receiving the income (the deductee). In 2026, with the integration of real-time tax tracking, these benefits are more significant than ever.

Benefits for the Deductor (Business/Employer)

Avoidance of Late Fees: Filing on time saves you from the mandatory late fee of ₹200 per day under Section 234E. This fee can quickly accumulate to equal the total tax amount deducted.

Prevention of Interest & Penalties: Timely filing ensures you aren't hit with additional interest (1.5% per month) on delayed deposits or heavy penalties (up to ₹1 Lakh) under Section 271H.

Business Expense Deduction: If you fail to deduct or file TDS, the Income Tax Department can disallow 30% of your business expenses. Filing correctly ensures your tax-deductible expenses are protected, lowering your overall tax bill.

Enhanced Financial Credibility: Clean TDS records are often required by banks for loan approvals and by government bodies for awarding tenders. It reflects high financial discipline and transparency.

Benefits for the Deductee (Employee/Vendor)

Seamless Tax Credit (Form 26AS/AIS): Your filing is what pushes the tax credit into the recipient’s Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS). Without your return, they cannot prove they have already paid tax on that income.

Faster Income Tax Refunds: If an employee or vendor is due a refund, they can only receive it once your TDS return is processed. Timely filing speeds up their refund cycle.

Ease of ITR Filing: When TDS data is pre-filled in their Income Tax Return (ITR), it reduces their administrative burden and prevents "mismatch" notices from the tax department.

Proof of Income: A TDS certificate (Form 16/16A) generated after a return filing serves as vital legal proof of income for visa applications and personal loans.

Eligible Criteria for TDS Return Filing

To file a TDS Return in 2026, you must meet the eligibility criteria for being a "Deductor." The responsibility to file lies entirely with the person or entity making the payment, provided the payment exceeds the government-defined threshold limits.

Who is Eligible (and Required) to File?

Under the Income Tax Act, the following entities are mandated to file TDS returns if they have deducted tax:

Corporate Entities: All private and public limited companies.

Government Offices: All central and state government departments/offices.

Individuals & HUFs (Business/Profession): Mandatory if your accounts were subject to a Tax Audit (u/s 44AB) in the preceding financial year.

Other Entities: Partnership firms, Association of Persons (AOP), Body of Individuals (BOI), and local authorities.

Transaction Eligibility (Thresholds for 2026)

You are only required to deduct tax and file a return if the payment crosses specific limits. Common examples include:

Salary (Section 192): If the employee's taxable income exceeds the basic exemption limit (₹3,00,000 under the New Tax Regime).

Professional Fees (Section 194J): If payments to a professional exceed ₹30,000 annually.

Rent (Section 194I): If total rent paid to a person exceeds ₹2,40,000 per year.

Contractual Payments (Section 194C): If a single payment exceeds ₹30,000 or aggregate annual payments exceed ₹1,00,000.

Purchase of Goods (Section 194Q): If purchases from a single vendor exceed ₹50 Lakhs in a year.

Essential Pre-requisites for Filing

Even if you meet the above, you cannot file the return without:

Valid TAN: A 10-digit alphanumeric Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number is mandatory. You cannot file using a PAN.

Deductee's PAN: You must have the PAN of the person you paid. If they don't provide a PAN, you must deduct tax at a higher rate (usually 20%).

Challan Details: Proof of the monthly TDS deposit (BSR Code, Date of Deposit, and Challan Serial Number).

Digital Signature (DSC): Required for online verification on the Income Tax portal for most corporate and audited assesses.

Documents Required for TDS Return Filing

To file a TDS Return in 2026, you don't typically upload physical documents. Instead, the process involves submitting a validated data file (FVU) generated from specific records.

Here is the checklist of documents and information you must have ready for the filing:

Gross Amount: The total amount paid/credited before deduction.

Date of Payment/Credit: The date the transaction occurred.

The Cap: The total late fee cannot exceed the total amount of TDS deducted for that specific quarter.

Requirement: You must pay this fee via Challan ITNS 281 before the portal will allow you to file the late return.

If you have deducted the tax but failed to deposit it with the government by the 7th of the following month, interest is charged monthly.

Late Deduction: 1% per month (or part of a month) from the date tax was deductible to the date it was actually deducted.

Late Deposit: 1.5% per month (or part of a month) from the date of deduction to the date of actual deposit.

Professional Tax Return Filling

OVERVIEW

Professional Tax (PT) Return Filing is a mandatory state-level compliance in India for individuals and businesses earning income through employment or trade. It involves reporting the tax deducted from employee salaries or paid by self-employed professionals to the respective state government. While governed by the state, the maximum annual tax per individual is constitutionally capped at ₹2,500.

The process primarily covers two certificates: PTRC, where employers deduct and file returns for their staff, and PTEC, where business owners and professionals pay for their own enrollment. Filing frequency (monthly, quarterly, or annual) and slab rates vary by state. Timely filing is crucial in 2026 to avoid steep interest and late fees, which can reach up to ₹5,000 or more depending on the state’s specific regulations.

Purpose of Professional Tax Return Filling

The purpose of Professional Tax (PT) Return Filing goes beyond just paying a tax; it is a mechanism for state governments to generate revenue for local development and a mandatory step for businesses to validate their statutory compliance.

The core reasons for filing PT returns in 2026 are:

Revenue for State Development

Unlike Income Tax, which goes to the Central Government, Professional Tax is a primary source of revenue for State Governments. The funds collected are specifically reserved for:

Improving local infrastructure (roads, water, and sanitation).

Funding welfare schemes and public health initiatives within the state.

Supporting municipal and local body activities.

Legal Proof of Compliance

Filing the return acts as a formal declaration that the employer or professional has fulfilled their legal duty. It provides:

Audit Readiness: During a labor law audit or a business inspection, PT return receipts are mandatory documents to prove you haven't defaulted.

Eligible Criteria for Professional Tax Return Filling

Professional Tax (PT) is a state-level tax in India levied on individuals earning an income through employment, profession, or trade. Since it is governed by individual state laws, the eligibility and filing rules vary by location, but the core principles remain the same.

Who is Eligible/Liable to Pay?

The liability to pay and file Professional Tax returns generally falls into three categories:

Salaried Individuals: If you are an employee, your employer is responsible for deducting the tax from your monthly salary and remitting it to the state government.

Self-Employed Professionals: If you practice a profession (e.g., Doctors, Lawyers, Chartered Accountants, Architects, Freelancers), you must register yourself and pay the tax directly.

Business Entities: Sole proprietorships, partnership firms, and private/public limited companies must register and pay PT both as an entity and on behalf of their employees.

Key Registration Certificates

There are two types of registrations you might need depending on your status:


| Certificate Type | Acronym | Who Needs It? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Professional Tax Enrolment Certificate | PTEC | Required by the business entity itself or a self-employed professional to pay their own tax. |
| Professional Tax Registration Certificate | PTRC | Required by an employer to deduct and remit tax from the salaries of their employees. |


Thresholds and Slabs (2026)

While each state has its own table, the Constitution of India limits the maximum Professional Tax to ₹2,500 per year.

Exemption Limit: Most states exempt individuals earning below a certain threshold (often ranging between ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 per month).

State Examples: * Maharashtra: Generally starts for males earning above ₹7,500/month; females earning up to ₹25,000/month are often exempt.

Karnataka: Usually applies to those earning above ₹25,000/month.

West Bengal: Slabs start from a monthly income of ₹10,001.

Exemptions

Certain groups are typically exempt from paying Professional Tax across most states:

Senior citizens (usually above 60 or 65 years).

Parents or guardians of children with mental or physical disabilities.

Persons with specific permanent physical disabilities (subject to medical certification).

Members of the armed forces (Army, Navy, Air Force) under certain state laws.

Filing Frequency and Deadlines

The "Return Filing" requirement mostly applies to employers (PTRC holders) and registered professionals (PTEC holders).

Monthly/Quarterly: If your tax liability is high (e.g., above ₹50,000/year in some states), you may need to file monthly returns.

Annual: Smaller businesses or self-employed individuals often file a single annual return.

Due Dates: Typically by the last day of the month for the previous month's tax, or a specific date in April/May for annual filers.

Exemptions from Professional Tax & Return Filing

While Professional Tax (PT) is mandatory for most income earners, state laws provide specific exemptions based on age, income level, and special circumstances. Understanding these is crucial, as being exempt from the tax doesn't always exempt you from the filing requirement.

General Exemptions (Common Across Most States)

Most Indian states (like Maharashtra, Karnataka, and West Bengal) exempt the following categories of individuals from paying PT:

Senior Citizens: Generally individuals aged 65 years and above (in Karnataka, the limit is 60 years).

Parents of Disabled Children: Parents or guardians of children with a physical or mental disability.

Armed Forces Personnel: Members of the Indian Army, Navy, and Air Force, including auxiliary forces and certain paramilitary forces (CRPF, BSF).

Do You Still Need to File Returns?

This is a common point of confusion. Whether you need to file a "Nil Return" depends on your registration status:

If you are NOT Registered: If you fall under an exemption category (e.g., you are a senior citizen or earn below the slab), you do not need to register for PT, and therefore, no return filing is required.

If you ARE Registered (PTRC/PTEC): If you already have a Professional Tax Registration, most states require you to file a return even if the tax amount is zero (Nil Return).

Example: If a company has 10 employees and all 10 earn below the taxable limit, the company (PTRC holder) must still file a monthly/annual Nil return to stay compliant.

Self-Employed (PTEC): In many states, if you are a PTEC holder (enrolled professional), you simply pay the annual tax. If you become exempt (e.g., turn 65), you should apply to cancel your registration to stop the filing requirement.

How to Claim an Exemption

Exemptions are not always automatic. To ensure you aren't penalized:

For Employees: Submit relevant proof (Disability Certificate, Age proof) to your HR/Payroll department. They will stop the deduction from your salary.

For Businesses/Professionals: If you are exempt, you should not apply for a PT certificate. If you already have one and are now exempt, login to the state’s tax portal (like Mahagst or e-Pragati) and file a Cancellation Application.
