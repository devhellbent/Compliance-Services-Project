GST Registration

OVERVIEW

GST registration in India is a mandatory digital process for businesses exceeding specific annual turnover thresholds. Currently, the limit is ₹40 lakh for goods suppliers and ₹20 lakh for service providers, though these are halved in "Special Category" states. Certain entities, such as e-commerce sellers and inter-state traders, must register regardless of turnover. Registration provides a unique GSTIN, enabling businesses to legally collect taxes, claim Input Tax Credits, and operate seamlessly across state borders.

The application is submitted via the official GST portal using a PAN card, Aadhaar, and business address proof. After generating a Temporary Reference Number (TRN) and completing e-KYC, the application undergoes official review. Once approved, typically within a few working days, a registration certificate is issued digitally. Failure to register when liable results in significant penalties, usually 10% of the tax due. Modern reforms have further streamlined this into a "Fast-Track" process to enhance ease of doing business

What is the GST ?

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a unified, destination-based indirect tax that replaced multiple levies like VAT and Service Tax in India. It is applied to the value added at each stage of the supply chain. Businesses utilize Input Tax Credits to avoid "tax on tax," ensuring transparency and efficiency.

The system is categorized into CGST, SGST, and IGST, depending on whether the transaction is within a state or across borders. By consolidating the tax structure into specific slabs (0% to 28%), GST creates a single national market, simplifying compliance for businesses nationwide.

Types of GST Registration in India: A Complete Guide

Choosing the right type of GST registration is a critical decision for every business in India. The classification depends primarily on your annual turnover, business model, and the nature of your operations. Here are the five main types of GST registration you need to know.

Normal Taxpayer (Regular Registration)

This is the most common category for most businesses operating in India.

Best for: Businesses with an annual turnover exceeding ₹40 Lakh (Goods) or ₹20 Lakh (Services).

Key Advantage: You can claim Input Tax Credit (ITC)—meaning you can deduct the tax paid on your business purchases from the tax you collect from customers.

Compliance: Requires regular monthly or quarterly filing (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B). There is no restriction on selling goods or services across state borders.

Composition Scheme

Designed to reduce the compliance burden for small-scale taxpayers.

Best for: Small traders, manufacturers, and restaurants with a turnover up to ₹1.5 Crore (₹50 Lakh for service providers).

Key Advantage: Pay a flat, lower tax rate (usually 1% to 6%) and file only one quarterly statement and one annual return.

Limitations: You cannot collect GST from customers, you cannot claim Input Tax Credits, and you are restricted to selling only within your home state (intra-state).

Casual Taxable Person (CTP)

This is a temporary registration for businesses that don't have a fixed place of operation in a specific state.

Best for: Seasonal businesses or individuals setting up temporary stalls at exhibitions, trade fairs, or pop-up events in a different state.

Validity: Valid for 90 days (can be extended once).

Requirement: You must pay an estimated tax amount in advance to the government before the registration is granted.

Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP)

Similar to the Casual Taxable Person, but specifically for entities outside of India.

Best for: Foreign individuals or businesses that occasionally supply goods or services to India but do not have a fixed office or branch here.

Requirement: Mandatory registration regardless of turnover. Like CTPs, they must provide an advance tax deposit and the registration is valid for a limited period (90 days).

Other Specialized Registrations

Depending on your specific role in the supply chain, you might fall into these technical categories:

Input Service Distributor (ISD): For head offices that receive tax invoices for services used by their branches; used only to distribute tax credits.

E-commerce Operator: Mandatory for platforms like Amazon, Flipkart, or Swiggy that facilitate sales for third-party sellers.

TDS/TCS Deductor: Specifically for government bodies (TDS) or e-commerce platforms (TCS) required to deduct/collect tax at the source.

Three-Component Structure of GST

In India, the GST follows a dual structure, allowing both the Central and State governments to levy and collect taxes simultaneously on a common base. This is managed through three primary components: CGST, SGST/UTGST, and IGST.

CGST (Central Goods and Services Tax)

Levied by: The Central Government.

Applicability: Only on Intra-state transactions (sales within the same state).

Revenue: The tax collected goes directly to the Central Treasury. It replaces old taxes like Central Excise Duty and Service Tax.

SGST / UTGST (State/Union Territory GST)

Levied by: The State Government (SGST) or Union Territory administration (UTGST).

Applicability: Only on Intra-state transactions.

Revenue: The tax collected goes to the respective State or Union Territory. It replaces taxes like VAT, Luxury Tax, and Entertainment Tax.

Note on Intra-state Sales: For a sale within a state (e.g., Mumbai to Pune), both CGST and SGST are charged. If the tax rate is 18%, it is split equally: 9% CGST and 9% SGST.

IGST (Integrated Goods and Services Tax)

Levied by: The Central Government.

Applicability: On Inter-state transactions (sales from one state to another) and Imports into India.

Revenue: The Center collects the tax and then shares it with the consuming state (where the goods or services end up).

Example: If a business in Delhi sells to a customer in Haryana at an 18% rate, only 18% IGST is charged on the invoice.

Without a GST number, your business is largely confined to your home state.

Interstate Sales: GST registration is mandatory for selling goods across state borders.

E-commerce Ready: To sell on platforms like Amazon, Flipkart, or Myntra, a GSTIN is a prerequisite. It allows you to tap into a nationwide customer base instantly.

GST replaced 17 different indirect taxes (like VAT, Service Tax, and Excise) with one single system.

You are not required to register for GST if:

You are an Agriculturist supplying produce grown directly from the land.

You supply non-taxable items like Petrol, Diesel, or Alcohol for human consumption (these are governed by State VAT/Excise).

GST Registration Timeline and Validity

Getting your GST registration certificate in 2026 is significantly faster than in previous years, thanks to the "GST 2.0" reforms. Here is the updated timeline and the rules regarding how long your registration stays valid.

Registration Timeline (How long it takes)

The time it takes to get your GSTIN (GST Identification Number) depends on the verification method you choose.

Fast-Track Approval (Rule 14A): ~3 Working Days Introduced for low-risk small businesses and professionals. If your estimated monthly tax liability is under ₹2.5 Lakh and you complete Aadhaar authentication, you can get approved in as little as 3 days.

Standard Online Approval: 7 Working Days This applies if you complete Aadhaar Authentication (OTP-based) and your application doesn't trigger any high-risk flags. Most regular businesses fall into this category.

With Physical Verification: ~30 Working Days If you opt out of Aadhaar authentication, or if the system flags your application for a "site visit," a tax officer will physically visit your business premises. The approval only comes after their report is submitted.

Biometric Authentication: +15 Days (if required) In certain states (like Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, and Puducherry), you may be asked to visit a GST Suvidha Kendra (GSK) for biometric verification. You have 15 days from the date of your TRN (Temporary Reference Number) to complete this.

Validity of the Certificate

Once issued, a GST Registration Certificate (Form REG-06) has different validity rules based on the type of taxpayer:

Regular Taxpayers: Lifetime (Perpetual) There is no expiry date. As long as you file your returns and don't violate any laws, the registration remains valid until you voluntarily cancel it or the department surrenders it.

Casual Taxable Persons: 90 Days For those setting up temporary stalls or seasonal businesses. This can be extended for another 90 days by applying through the portal before the original period expires.

Non-Resident Taxable Persons: 90 Days Similar to casual taxpayers, this is temporary and must be extended if the business activity continues.

Critical Deadlines Post-Registration

Getting the certificate is only the first step. To keep it active, you must follow these 2026 mandates:

Bank Account Linking: You must add your business bank account details to the GST portal within 30 days of getting your GSTIN.

Warning: Failure to do this will result in automatic suspension of your registration, meaning you won't be able to issue invoices or file returns.

Displaying GSTIN: You are legally required to display your GST registration certificate and your GSTIN on a signboard at your "Principal Place of Business."

First Return: Your first GST return must include all sales/purchases made from the date you became liable for registration until the date the certificate was granted.

Additional Place of Business in GST

OVERVIEW

An Additional Place of Business (APOB) is any location, such as a warehouse, branch office, or depot, where you conduct business activities within the same state as your primary registration. For e-commerce sellers, this is essential for legal compliance when using third-party fulfillment centers like Amazon FBA or Flipkart Assured.

Registering an APOB allows you to store inventory legally and claim Input Tax Credit on expenses incurred at that location. You do not need a new GSTIN; you simply update your existing certificate via the GST portal by providing address proof and a No Objection Certificate (NOC) for the new premises.

Under the GST Law, the 'Place of Business' is defined broadly to include any location where you conduct your commercial activities. It is not just limited to where you sit and work; it covers every link in your supply chain.

What is ‘Place of Business’ under the GST Law?

According to Section 2(85) of the CGST Act, a Place of Business includes:

Operational Hubs: Any place where the business is ordinarily carried on, including a warehouse, a godown, or any other place where a taxable person stores their goods, supplies, or receives goods or services.

Administrative Offices: Any place where a taxable person maintains their books of accounts.

Agent Locations: Any place where a taxable person is engaged in business through an agent, by whatever name called.

Two Key Classifications

To keep things organized, the GST portal categorizes these into two types:

Principal Place of Business (PPOB): This is the primary location specified in your registration certificate (usually your head office or registered company address). This is where your main accounts are kept and where the GST department sends official correspondence.

Additional Place of Business (APOB): Any other location (like a branch, factory, or a marketplace fulfillment center) within the same state that is used for business but is not the primary office.

PPOB vs APOB


| Feature | Principal Place of Business (PPOB) | Additional Place of Business (APOB) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definition | The primary location of the business, usually the head office or registered address. | Any other location (branch, warehouse, factory) where business is conducted. |
| Quantity | You can have only one PPOB per state per GSTIN. | You can have multiple APOBs under the same GSTIN in that state. |
| Books of Account | Statutory books and records must be maintained here by law. | Records for specific activities at this site may be kept here or at the PPOB. |
| Communication | All official notices and legal letters from the GST department are sent here. | Generally not used for official legal correspondence. |
| Registration | Declared at the time of the initial GST application. | Added later via a "Non-Core Amendment" on the GST portal. |
| Examples | Your main office, home office, or registered company headquarters. | Amazon/Flipkart warehouses, godowns, or secondary retail outlets. |

Here are the primary benefits of APOB registration:

A GST Registration Amendment is the formal process of updating your business details on the GST portal to ensure legal compliance. This ensures your GST certificate remains an accurate reflection of your current business status.

Amendments are categorized into Core Fields, which require officer approval (like address or ownership changes), and Non-Core Fields, which are updated instantly (like contact details). You must file these changes via Form GST REG-14 within 15 days of the change to avoid penalties.

Stakeholders: Adding or deleting Directors, Partners, Promoters, or the Karta of a HUF.

Nature of Business Activity: Changing the type of work done at a location (e.g., from "Retail" to "Wholesale").

Amending your GST registration is a double-edged sword: it is a legal necessity that keeps your business running smoothly, but it also invites official scrutiny. Here is a breakdown of the advantages and disadvantages.

GSTR-2B Statement: This is auto-generated on the portal. You must match your purchase bills with this statement to ensure your suppliers have filed their taxes.

TCS Reconciliation Data

Since platforms deduct 1% TCS from your sales, you need:

Bank Statements: To verify the final settlements received after all deductions (TCS, commissions, and shipping).

Final Data Reconciliation

GSTR-10

OVERVIEW

GSTR-10, known as the Final Return, is a mandatory one-time filing for taxpayers whose GST registration has been surrendered or cancelled. Unlike the annual GSTR-9, this return ensures that all remaining tax liabilities on held stock and capital goods are settled before the business formally exits the GST regime.

The return must be filed within three months of the cancellation date or the issuance of the cancellation order, whichever is later. Failure to file GSTR-10 results in a late fee of ₹200 per day, capped at a maximum of ₹10,000.

Purpose of the GSTR-10 Return Filing

The primary purpose of GSTR-10 is to act as a "final settlement" between the taxpayer and the government. It ensures that no tax-paid benefits (Input Tax Credit) remain with a business once it exits the GST network.

Here is why filing GSTR-10 is critical:

Closing the Tax Credit Loop

When you buy goods for business, you claim Input Tax Credit (ITC). If you close your business while still holding that stock, the government requires you to "pay back" that credit. GSTR-10 captures the details of:

Inputs held in stock (Raw materials).

Inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods.

Capital goods or plant and machinery.

Declaring Final Liabilities

It serves as a formal declaration of any tax due on the assets remaining with the business on the date of cancellation. You must pay an amount equal to the ITC involved in those stocks or the output tax on the market value of those goods, whichever is higher.

Preventing Revenue Loss

Without GSTR-10, a taxpayer could theoretically buy large amounts of stock, claim the ITC to offset other taxes, and then vanish by cancelling their registration. This return ensures the government recovers the tax benefit on any unsold items.

Legal Discharge of GSTIN

Filing GSTR-10 is the final step in the "de-registration" process. Successfully submitting this return:

Formally closes your record in the GST database.

Prevents future automatic notices for non-filing.

Protects the taxpayer from heavy penalties and legal recovery actions later.

Importance of GSTR-10 Return Filing

Filing the GSTR-10 (Final Return) is more than just a procedural hurdle; it is the legal "handshake" that officially terminates your relationship with the GST department. Without it, your GST account remains in a state of limbo, which can lead to significant financial and legal headaches.

Here is why filing GSTR-10 is critically important:

Legal Closure of GSTIN

Even if your cancellation application is approved, your GST compliance cycle is not complete until GSTR-10 is filed. Failing to submit this return keeps your GSTIN "active" for scrutiny purposes, meaning the tax department can still issue notices for non-compliance years later.

Avoidance of Heavy Late Fees

The GST portal is programmed to trigger a late fee of ₹200 per day the moment the three-month deadline passes.

This fee accumulates automatically.

It is capped at ₹10,000.

You cannot "close" your record until this penalty is cleared, which can be a frustrating expense for a business that has already stopped operating.

Protection from "Best Judgment Assessment"

If you fail to file GSTR-10, the tax authorities have the power to conduct a "Best Judgment Assessment" (under Section 62).

They can estimate your tax liability based on your previous data.

They may issue a demand order (Form GST ASMT-13) for a much higher amount than you actually owe.

Filing GSTR-10 allows you to declare your actual stock and liability, maintaining control over the narrative.

Reconciliation of Input Tax Credit (ITC)

The Principle: You cannot keep the tax benefit (ITC) for goods you haven't sold to a customer. By filing GSTR-10, you accurately calculate and pay back the ITC on:

Raw materials and semi-finished goods in your warehouse.

Capital goods (like laptops or machinery), where the tax is paid back proportionately based on the remaining useful life of the asset.

Smooth Transition for Future Ventures

If you plan to start a new business or apply for a new GST registration in the future, a "Defaulting" status on a previous GSTIN can be a major red flag.

A clean exit via GSTR-10 ensures your PAN-based compliance record remains untarnished.

It prevents the department from blocking your new registration due to "outstanding returns" from an old entity.

GST Revocation

OVERVIEW

GST Revocation is the legal process to restore a GST registration that was cancelled by tax authorities (Suo Moto), typically due to non-filing of returns. It allows businesses to reactivate their original GSTIN rather than applying for a new one. This process is only available if the cancellation was initiated by an officer; it cannot be used if the taxpayer voluntarily surrendered their registration. It ensures business continuity and allows the taxpayer to reclaim any "frozen" Input Tax Credit.

To apply, taxpayers must file Form GST REG-21 within 90 days of the cancellation order, though extensions up to 270 days are possible with approval. Before applying, all pending returns must be filed and outstanding taxes, interest, and late fees must be cleared. If the tax officer is satisfied with the compliance, they issue an order in Form GST REG-22, officially restoring the GST status to "Active" and enabling the business to resume operations legally.

Purpose and Reason of GST Revocation

The Purpose of GST Revocation is to provide a "second chance" to businesses whose GST registration has been forcefully cancelled by the government, allowing them to restore their original tax identity and resume operations without losing their business history or tax credits.

The Core Purpose

Business Continuity: Restores the existing GSTIN so you don't have to change your tax ID on existing contracts, bank accounts, or branding.

Recovering Frozen Credits: It is the only way to unlock and utilize the Input Tax Credit (ITC) that was sitting in your electronic ledger at the time of cancellation.

Legal Compliance: It allows a business to "regularize" its status. Operating under a cancelled GSTIN is illegal and attracts heavy penalties (often 100% of the tax due).

Common Reasons for Revocation

Revocation is only possible if the cancellation was initiated "Suo Moto" (on its own motion) by a Tax Officer. The common triggers that lead to a need for revocation include:


| Reason for Cancellation | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Non-Filing of Returns | Most common reason. For regular taxpayers, failing to file returns for a continuous period of 6 months. |
| Composition Default | If a Composition Dealer fails to file returns for 3 consecutive tax periods. |
| No Business Activity | Taking a voluntary registration but failing to commence business within 6 months. |
| Compliance Breach | Violating specific GST rules, such as not displaying the GSTIN at the place of business or failing to update bank details. |
| Fraudulent Intent | Obtaining registration through fraud, willful misstatement, or suppression of facts. |


Why Revocation over a New Registration?

While you could technically apply for a fresh registration, revocation is strategically better because:

Avoids "Blacklisting": If you apply for a new GSTIN while an old one is cancelled due to defaults, the system may flag your PAN, leading to delays or rejection of the new application.

Saves Money: Applying for a new registration doesn't clear the old penalties. Revocation requires you to pay those dues anyway, but it restores your ability to use old ITC to pay them off.

Customer Trust: Many corporate clients will not deal with a vendor whose GST history shows a "Cancelled" status, as they cannot claim ITC on purchases from them.

Time Limit for Revocation of GST Registration

In India, the time limit for filing an application for the revocation of GST registration has been significantly expanded to provide taxpayers more breathing room. As of 2026, the timelines are governed by the amendments introduced to Section 30 of the CGST Act.

Here is the breakdown of the allowed timeframes:

The Standard Window (90 Days)

You must file Form GST REG-21 within 90 days from the date the cancellation order was served on you.

Historical Note: This was originally only 30 days, but the GST Council increased it to 90 days to reduce the litigation burden on small businesses.

The Extension Window (Additional 180 Days)

If you miss the initial 90-day deadline, you can still apply for an extension. The law allows for a further extension of up to 180 days, bringing the total possible time to 270 days (9 months) from the date of the cancellation order.

Approval Authority: This extension is not automatic. It requires approval from the Commissioner or an officer authorized by them (typically not below the rank of Additional or Joint Commissioner).

Requirement: You must demonstrate "sufficient cause" (a valid reason) for why the application could not be filed within the first 90 days.

What if you cross the 270-day limit?

Once the 270-day window closes, the GST portal generally blocks the revocation functionality for that GSTIN. At this stage, your only legal remedies are:

File an Appeal: File an appeal before the Appellate Authority (Form GST APL-01) within 3 months of the cancellation order.

High Court: If the appeal period has also lapsed, taxpayers often file a Writ Petition in the High Court, which has the power to direct the department to restore the registration in exceptional cases

Eligible Criteria for GST Revocation

To be eligible for GST revocation, specific conditions must be met regarding the nature of the cancellation, the status of your tax filings, and the timeline of your application.

Here are the four primary eligibility criteria for restoring a cancelled GSTIN

Source of Cancellation (Suo Moto)

You are only eligible for revocation if your registration was cancelled by a GST Officer on their own motion (known as Suo Moto cancellation).

Eligible: Cancellation due to non-filing of returns, fraud, or non-commencement of business.

Ineligible: If you voluntarily applied for cancellation (e.g., due to business closure or turnover falling below the limit), you cannot use the revocation process.

Clearance of Compliance Backlog

If your registration was cancelled due to non-filing of returns (the most common reason), the GST portal will physically block your revocation application until you complete the following:

Filing of Returns: You must file all pending returns (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, etc.) up to the date of the cancellation order.

Payment of Dues: All outstanding tax liabilities, including interest, penalties, and late fees, must be paid in full.

Understanding Rule 23: Revocation of Cancelled GST Registration

Rule 23 of the CGST Rules, 2017, is the procedural engine that powers the revocation process. It bridges the gap between the law (Section 30) and the actual steps a taxpayer must take on the GST portal to reactivate a Suo Moto cancelled registration.

Here is a breakdown of Rule 23 as it stands in 2026:

The Core Mandate (Rule 23(1))

If an officer cancels your registration on their own motion, you have the right to submit Form GST REG-21.

The "Clearance" Rule: You cannot file for revocation if the reason for cancellation was non-filing of returns, unless you first file those returns and pay all taxes, interest, and late fees. The portal effectively locks the "Submit" button until the system detects these filings

Standard and Extended Timelines

Rule 23 was amended (effective from late 2023) to lengthen the timeframes:

Standard Period: 90 days from the date the cancellation order was served.

Extension: The Commissioner (or an authorized Additional/Joint Commissioner) can extend this by another 180 days if you show "sufficient cause."

Total Window: A maximum of 270 days.

The Officer’s Decision (Rule 23(2))

Once you submit your application, the Proper Officer has 30 days to act:

Approval: If satisfied, the officer issues Form GST REG-22, restoring your status.

Rejection: If not satisfied, the officer must follow a specific "due process" before rejecting your request. They cannot reject it outright without giving you a chance to explain.

The "Show Cause" Process (Rule 23(3) & 23(4))

Notice (REG-23): If the officer intends to reject your application, they must issue a notice in Form GST REG-23.

Your Reply (REG-24): You must respond within 7 working days using Form GST REG-24.

Final Disposal: After receiving your reply, the officer must make a final decision (Accept or Reject) within another 30 days.

Documentation Required for Reactivating GST Registration

Reactivating a GST registration that has been cancelled by the department (Suo Moto) requires a formal application through Form GST REG-21. Since the portal only allows this after compliance gaps are filled, your "documentation" is split between digital proofs and physical uploads.

Here is the updated documentation checklist for 2026:

Digital Pre-requisites (System Verified)

Before the portal even lets you submit the revocation form, the following must be digitally updated:

Filing Confirmation (ARNs): You must have filed all pending returns (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B) from the date of default up to the date of cancellation.

Payment Receipts (Challans): Proof that all taxes, interest (18%), and late fees (₹200/day, capped at ₹10,000 per return) have been paid.

Mandatory Uploads for Form GST REG-21

While the form is filed online, you should attach the following documents in PDF/JPEG format:

Cancellation Order (GST REG-19): A copy of the original order issued by the officer.

Detailed Reason for Revocation: A formal letter on your business letterhead explaining why the returns were not filed (e.g., medical emergency, financial crisis, or technical issues).

Latest Proof of Business Address: A recent electricity bill, municipal khata copy, or a registered rent agreement (to prove the business is still operating from the declared place).

Bank Account Proof: A cancelled cheque or a recent bank statement showing the business name and account number.

Identity & Verification Documents

Aadhaar Authentication: It is now mandatory for the authorized signatory to have completed Aadhaar authentication on the portal.

PAN of the Entity: A copy of the PAN card of the proprietor, partners, or the company.

Digital Signature Certificate (DSC): Mandatory for Companies and LLPs. For individuals/partnerships, an EVC (OTP) is sufficient.

Additional Documents (If applying for Extension)

If you are applying after the 90-day mark (but before 270 days), you need:

Condonation of Delay Application: A specific request letter addressed to the Commissioner/Joint Commissioner explaining the delay in applying for revocation.

Supporting Evidence for Delay: If the delay was due to illness, attach medical certificates; if it was due to a death in the family, attach the death certificate.

Fees and Penalties for GST Revocation

In India, there is no direct government fee for filing the application for revocation (Form GST REG-21). The "cost" of revocation actually comes from clearing the compliance backlog that caused the cancellation in the first place.

Here is the breakdown of the financial obligations you must fulfill to reactivate your GSTIN in 2026:

Late Fees (For Pending Returns)

If your registration was cancelled for non-filing, you must file all past-due returns. The late fees for these returns are:

Normal Returns (GSTR-3B/GSTR-1): ₹50 per day (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST).

Nil Returns: ₹20 per day (₹10 CGST + ₹10 SGST).

Maximum Cap: Generally capped at ₹10,000 per return (₹5,000 CGST + ₹5,000 SGST).

Interest on Delayed Tax

If you had a tax liability during the period your returns were not filed:

Rate: 18% per annum.

Calculation: Calculated from the original due date of the return until the actual date of payment.

Penalties (Specific Offenses)

While there is no "revocation penalty," the officer may have levied penalties under Section 122 or Section 125 during the cancellation proceedings:

Standard Penalty: Typically ₹10,000 or 10% of the tax due (whichever is higher) for significant defaults.

General Penalty: Up to ₹25,000 for other contraventions where no specific penalty is mentioned.

Professional Fees (Optional)

If you hire a CA, Tax Practitioner, or an AI-led service to handle the documentation, reply to notices (REG-24), or follow up with the jurisdictional officer:

Range: Usually between ₹1,000 to ₹5,000, depending on the complexity and number of pending returns.
